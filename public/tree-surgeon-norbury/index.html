SW16 is not the same as Croydon

Norbury sits where Croydon meets its neighbours. SW16 and CR7 addresses can fall under different councils, and the Croydon-Merton boundary forms the western edge of the Norbury Estate. The Northborough Recreation Ground, just west of Darcy Road, is in Merton, not Croydon.

Why this matters for your tree:

  • Tree preservation orders and conservation area notices are handled by the council the property is in, not by the postcode.
  • Croydon's rules, fines and phone numbers in this page apply to Croydon addresses.
  • We work in the London Borough of Croydon. If your address might be across the line, give us your full address and we confirm before we quote.

The Norbury Estate Conservation Area

Croydon Council designated the Norbury Estate as a conservation area in December 2008 and extended it in 2014 to include 254 to 278 Northborough Road. The streets form a grid inside a rectangle of Tylecroft Road, Palmers Road, Northborough Road and Darcy Road, with Newlands Road running through as the spine. London Road is the eastern edge.

What it is. A cottage estate on land bought in 1901, with 642 homes built by March 1920. All are two-storey terraces in an Edwardian cottage style, in groups of 8, 10, 15 or 20.

What it means for trees.

  • Notice first. Croydon needs six weeks' written notice, free of charge, before most pruning or felling, and unauthorised work risks a heavy fine.
  • The council's appraisal lists loss of trees and planting as a threat to the area's character, so expect a question about replacement if you remove a front-garden tree.
  • Trees at the back that show from the street count too: the appraisal says front-garden trees and visible rear-garden trees add to the streetscape.

If you live elsewhere in Norbury, check the council's map. Outside the estate, a TPO can still protect a tree. See conservation area tree work and tree preservation orders.

Pollarded street trees and small tree pits

The appraisal says Northborough Road and Tylecroft Road are tree-lined with a mix of species including cherry, while other streets are more sparsely planted. Tree pits are small, some edged with granite setts, and many mature street trees have been heavily pollarded. The council has also run a recent planting programme.

  • Street trees are the council's. Report a tree that is damaged, leaning or blocking light through its street-reporting app. Do not prune it yourself.
  • Pollards need regular cutting back, which is the council's job on the street. In your own garden, a pollarded tree is a long-term commitment. See tree pruning for how to pollard properly and what to avoid.
  • Roots and pavements. Small pits in tight terrace streets are where roots and paving meet. See trees near buildings.

Working in a Norbury Estate terrace

The estate is built in tight groups, which affects the work:

  • Narrow plots. Houses are about 3.5 to 4 m wide in the east, with rear gardens around 14 m deep.
  • Gaps of 2.5 to 3.5 m between terraces can allow a narrow-access machine, but not a vehicle.
  • No side access for most. Equipment often has to go through the house.
  • A slope in places. Part of the estate sits on the Pollards Hill ridge.
  • Sectional dismantling is the norm for removals, with ropes to lower pieces into a small garden.

A back-door photo and your narrowest measurement let us plan the kit. See tree removal and stump grinding for how narrow-access jobs are done.

Norbury Hall Park and nearby gardens

Norbury Hall Park, off Craignish Avenue, is a council-run park of about 3 hectares with specimen trees including oak, deodar cedar, yew, dawn redwood, horse chestnut and red oak, set within acid grassland. It is a designated local wildlife site. If your garden backs onto it, the land and trees beyond the fence are the council's. Photograph and report anything that comes over, and see who is responsible for a storm-damaged tree. Mature specimen trees also bring bats and nesting birds, so we check before cutting from March to August.

Parking

Many Norbury roads have controlled parking, and the estate's terraces have no off-street space for a lorry. Expect us to book a council bay suspension about two weeks before the job.

Tree Surgeon in Norbury, SW16 and CR7: frequently asked questions

Is Norbury a conservation area?

Part of it is. The Norbury Estate Conservation Area covers the cottage estate around Tylecroft Road, Palmers Road, Northborough Road, Darcy Road and Newlands Road. Elsewhere in Norbury, check your address with the council.

Is my SW16 address in Croydon?

Not necessarily. SW16 crosses borough lines, and the council for your property decides the tree rules. Give us your full address.

Do I need permission to prune a tree on the Norbury Estate?

You must notify Croydon Council six weeks beforehand, in writing. It costs nothing, and we draft the notice.

Why are the street trees pollarded?

The council's appraisal records that mature street trees in the estate are heavily pollarded and sit in small tree pits.

Who do I tell about a street tree that is damaged?

Croydon Council, through its street-reporting app. Ring 999 if the road is blocked or there is immediate danger.

Can you remove a tree with no side access?

Yes, by dismantling it in sections and carrying everything through the house with floors protected.

What does tree work cost in a Norbury terrace?

Mostly £200 to £600, because the access is tight and everything is hand-carried. A big tree over a roof costs more.

Can I cut my neighbour's overhanging tree?

Only back to the boundary, and in a conservation area the notice rule still applies.

What if a tree falls outside your hours?

Our diary runs Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm. For urgent danger, see the numbers on our emergency page.
