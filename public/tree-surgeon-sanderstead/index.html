Is your tree protected? Rules in Sanderstead

We found no conservation area covering Sanderstead itself, but Croydon Council has named Sanderstead Village among nine areas it would consider for conservation area status, and its Cabinet agreed a list of potential new areas in December 2025. Until one is designated, the main protection for private trees is a tree preservation order. If it is designated, Croydon's six-week notice rule would apply to trees within it. Check the council's current list for your road.

Croydon Council also says a TPO stops visually important trees being removed or pruned without formal permission. Email development.management@croydon.gov.uk with the tree's details, or send us your address. See the TPO guide.

Trees and woodland around Sanderstead

Sanderstead Plantation, on Addington Road, is a woodland of about 8.8 hectares owned by Croydon Council. The council describes it as predominantly beech, with oak, horse chestnut, sweet chestnut and sycamore, and noted for its bluebells in May. The London Parks and Gardens Trust lists it as a Site of Nature Conservation Importance in the Green Belt.

Sanderstead Pond (and Green) and All Saints Churchyard are on Croydon's local list of historic parks and gardens, which is a heritage record rather than a tree control.

Maps and records describe public sites and one or two documented areas, not every garden. Check your own tree with the council.

Access, parking and neighbours

Gardens near the Plantation may back onto council land, so check who owns the boundary strip before any work, and expect extra wildlife checks.

If a vehicle or chipper needs road space, Croydon Council offers a dispensation to park on yellow lines while work is carried out, or a suspension of a parking bay. It asks for at least five working days' notice, payment first and a separate licence for a skip on the road. Check current fees on the council's page.

Tell us if a tree is on a boundary or overhangs next door. You may be able to cut branches back to the boundary but not beyond it, and cuttings should be offered back. Work only goes ahead with the tree owner's consent.
