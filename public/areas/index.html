Which Council's Tree Rules Apply to You?

Tree protection is decided by the council for your address, not by the name of the area. Getting this wrong can mean a fine, so we check it first.

For addresses in Croydon, Croydon Council says a tree preservation order stops visually important trees being removed or pruned without formal permission, and that you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area. Fines of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting down a protected tree without permission, and up to £2,500 for pruning one.

Not sure which council you're in? Send us your full address and a photo of the tree. We'll check the council, whether a TPO applies and whether you're in a conservation area, before we quote.

What's Different About Trees Across Croydon?

North and south have different ground. Broadly, the north of the borough (Norbury, Thornton Heath, Upper Norwood and Crystal Palace) sits on clay soil that shrinks in dry weather, while the southern hills (Purley, Kenley, Coulsdon, Sanderstead and Selsdon) sit on chalk. That affects subsidence risk, root behaviour and how easy it is to grind a stump. See trees near buildings.

Different housing, different access. Terraces in the north often mean narrow passages and no vehicle access. The southern suburbs usually have larger gardens, but also slopes and steep drives.

Conservation areas and TPOs cluster. Several areas have named conservation areas, such as the East India Estate in Addiscombe, the Norbury Estate, the Webb Estate and Upper Woodcote Village in Purley, and Croham Manor Road in South Croydon, which is why we check first.

Woodland and wildlife. The southern edge of the borough sits close to commons, woods and the North Downs, so nesting birds and bats are a regular consideration. See our nesting season guide.

Ash dieback. Ash is common in the woods and gardens of the south, so we assess ash trees before any felling. See our ash dieback guide.

Tree Surgeon Areas: FAQs

Which areas do you cover?

We cover the London Borough of Croydon, including Addiscombe, Shirley, Thornton Heath, Norbury, Crystal Palace and Upper Norwood, South Croydon, Selsdon, Sanderstead, Purley, Coulsdon and Kenley.

Do you cover my postcode?

We cover CR0, CR2, CR5, CR7 and CR8, plus SE19 for Upper Norwood and Crystal Palace and SW16 for Norbury. Send us your postcode if you're not sure.

Do you cover Sutton, Mitcham, Caterham, Bromley or other nearby areas?

Not as standard. Call us and we'll tell you whether we can help.

How do I know which council's tree rules apply to me?

It depends on your address. Most of our areas fall under Croydon Council, but Norbury and Upper Norwood sit near borough boundaries. Send us your address and we'll check.

Is my tree protected?

It might be, if it has a TPO or is in a conservation area. We check as part of every quote.

Do prices differ between areas?

Mainly because of access, tree size and what's near the tree, not the postcode. See our cost calculator.

Can you work on trees in a conservation area?

Yes, with six weeks' written notice to the council for trees in a Croydon conservation area. We prepare and submit the notice for you.

Do you work on council or street trees?

No. Street and park trees belong to the council. Report problems to the council.

Do you offer emergency tree work in all areas?

Yes, across the eleven areas. We respond to emergencies, Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm. See emergency tree work.

Which area page should I read?

The one closest to you. Each covers the local trees, access and council rules for that area.
