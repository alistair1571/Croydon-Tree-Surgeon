The Croydon rules in brief

Cutting the wrong tree is an expensive mistake, so every quote starts with a check. This is what Croydon Council itself says.

SituationWhat Croydon Council says
The tree has a TPOYou need consent before pruning or felling it. There is currently no application fee
Your home is in a conservation areaSix weeks' written notice before working on trees. Croydon has 21 conservation areas, and nine more are being considered
The tree is deadNo formal permission is needed under a TPO
The tree is dyingFormal consent is still required
The tree is dangerousOnly urgent, necessary work can go ahead without consent, and the council must receive five days' notice
Cutting without permissionFines of up to £20,000 for destroying or felling a protected tree, and up to £2,500 for pruning one

How to find out if your tree is protected. Croydon's TPO page offers no postcode lookup. It asks you to email development.management@croydon.gov.uk or ring 020 8726 6800 with the tree's details. We do that for you with every quote.

Check again just before the work. A new TPO takes effect the moment it is made and must be confirmed within six months, so last spring's answer may be out of date.

The full detail is in our guides to tree preservation orders, tree work in a conservation area and whether you need permission to fell a tree.

What moves the price. Size and condition, access (a terraced house with a narrow side return takes longer than a drive with a clear view), whether the tree must be taken down in sections, what happens to the logs and chip, and whether a TPO or conservation notice has to be dealt with first. Our cost guide goes through each.

The Croydon extra most quotes forget: parking. If the van and chipper cannot park near the tree, a bay suspension may be needed. Croydon Council asks for at least 10 working days' notice and payment first. Its published charges start at £35 per bay per day for residents and £57 for commercial users, plus an admin charge, so check the current fees before you book. That notice period is also a reason to ask about dates early.

What a good written quote shows. The address and the tree or trees, the work in plain words (for example "crown reduce by up to 2 m", not just "trim"), whether stumps, logs and brash are taken away, VAT, who handles any TPO or conservation notice, and proof of insurance on request.

A tree down or dangerous right now

  1. People and pets first. Get everyone clear, and ring 999 if anyone is hurt or in danger.
  2. Power lines. Treat any line near a tree as live. UK Power Networks says to stay at least 10 metres from damaged or grounded equipment and to ring 105 (or 0800 31 63 105), open 24 hours. Add 999 if the public is at significant risk.
  3. Road, railway or gas. A fallen street tree is the council's to deal with, and Croydon points people to the Love Clean Streets app. Use 999 if a road is blocked. If a tree is on railway land, stay off the track and tell Network Rail on 03457 11 41 41. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 0800 111 999.
  4. Photograph, then ring your insurer before anything is moved.
  5. Then ring us. We make the tree safe and clear the site.

The full walk-through is on our emergency tree work page, and storm-damaged tree: who is responsible? covers liability and insurance.

Signs a standing tree is becoming a risk: a sudden new lean or lifted soil at the base, cracks or a split between main stems, large dead branches, fungus at the foot or on the trunk, cavities, and ash trees with thin crowns and dying shoots. Get it looked at before the next storm, not after. See ash dieback for Croydon owners.

Whose tree is it? Neighbours, council and landlords

Tree disputes usually come down to one question: who owns the trunk?

  • A tree in your garden. It is yours, and so is the responsibility if it causes damage.
  • A neighbour's overhanging branches. Under common law, as councils such as Test Valley explain, you can cut back branches that overhang your land, but only to the boundary, only the minimum needed, and without stepping onto their land. You must dispose of the cuttings and not dump them next door. You cannot reduce the height of their tree, and a protected tree still needs consent first. A friendly word first prevents most arguments. This is not legal advice.
  • A tall leylandii or conifer hedge next door. Talk to them first. If that fails, the council can consider a high hedge complaint about evergreen hedges blocking light to your home. See hedge cutting in Croydon.
  • A tree on the pavement or in a park. Croydon Council has said it looks after around 32,000 street trees. Report problems to the council, and never prune or fell one yourself.
  • You rent. Tell your landlord or agent in writing. The owner's written permission is needed before any tree comes down, and protected-tree rules still apply.

Birds, bats and trees near buildings

Wildlife. Active birds' nests are protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. The law sets no fixed calendar, but most nesting falls between March and August, so we plan around it and check for nests before cutting. Bat roosts are protected even when no bats are home, and work stops until the right advice is taken. See tree and hedge work in nesting season.

Near a house. Heavy limbs over a roof are usually reduced in stages and lowered, not dropped. A tree is not automatically to blame for a drain problem, so get a drain survey first. Across much of London the ground is clay that shrinks and swells, and removing a large tree beside a house can itself cause movement, so talk to your insurer or a structural engineer before you take one out. See trees near buildings and tree surveys.

Tree Preservation Orders (TPOs)

If your tree has a TPO, you generally need written consent from Croydon Council before cutting down, topping, lopping or uprooting it. Breaching a TPO is a criminal offence: fines can reach £20,000 in the magistrates' court, and more if the case goes to the Crown Court. We check the council's records for you at no cost. See our TPO guide.

Conservation areas

Croydon has several conservation areas, including parts of Purley, South Croydon, Upper Norwood and Addington. In a conservation area, trees are protected even without a TPO. You must give the council six weeks' written notice before cutting down or pruning a tree with a trunk over 75mm in diameter, measured 1.5m above ground. We prepare and submit the notice for you. See our conservation area guide.

Dead, dying or dangerous trees

There are exemptions, but they're narrower than most people think. Dead or immediately dangerous trees can often be dealt with, but in a conservation area you still need to give five working days' notice first, and you should keep photographic evidence. Don't assume. Ask us.

Nesting birds and bats

It's illegal to disturb nesting birds, typically from March to August, or to harm bat roosts. This can affect timing even when the tree has no other protection. See our nesting season guide.

Council-owned and neighbours' trees

Street and park trees belong to the council, so you can't touch them. Report problems directly to Croydon Council. If a neighbour's tree overhangs your garden, you can cut back branches to the boundary, but you must offer the cuttings back and you can't harm the tree. If you're in a conservation area or the tree has a TPO, the rules above still apply. For problem evergreen hedges over 2m tall, the High Hedges complaint route through the council may apply.

Planning conditions

Trees on newer developments are often protected by planning conditions, even with no TPO, so check before you cut. See Do I need permission to fell a tree?

Tree surgeon Croydon: frequently asked questions

Are you insured and qualified?

We hold £5 million public liability insurance and our team is NPTC/LANTRA qualified. Ask for proof when you get your quote, and ask the same of anyone else you speak to. Our guide on how to choose a tree surgeon lists what to check.

Do you take the waste away?

Yes, branches are chipped and cleared as part of the job unless the quote says otherwise. Logs and stumps are the usual exceptions, so say if you want to keep the wood.

Do I have to grind out the stump after tree removal?

No, but a stump left in the ground can regrow, rot unevenly and get in the way of a lawn, patio or new planting. If you are replanting in the same spot, grinding is usually worth doing. See stump grinding in Croydon.

How long does tree work take?

A small pruning job can be done in part of a day, while a large tree in a tight garden can take longer. We give you a realistic estimate once we know the tree, the access and any paperwork.

Can I get a quote from photos?

Often, yes. Send clear photos of the whole tree, the base, and what is nearby, plus the postcode. Complicated jobs may still need a visit before the price is fixed.

Can I use a tree surgeon if I rent my home?

Yes, with the landlord's or agent's written permission. Protected-tree and conservation area rules apply in the same way.

What is the difference between a tree surgeon and a gardener?

A tree surgeon climbs or uses specialist equipment to work on large trees safely, and also understands protection rules and wildlife law. A gardener is suited to light hedge trimming and small shrubs.

Do you cover my road?

We cover the whole of the London Borough of Croydon. If your address is on the edge, give us the postcode.
