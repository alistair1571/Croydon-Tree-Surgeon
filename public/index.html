Tree Preservation Orders (TPOs)

If your tree has a TPO, you generally need written consent from Croydon Council before cutting down, topping, lopping or uprooting it. Breaching a TPO is a criminal offence: fines can reach £20,000 in the magistrates' court, and more if the case goes to the Crown Court. We check the council's records for you at no cost. See our TPO guide.

Conservation areas

Croydon has several conservation areas, including parts of Purley, South Croydon, Upper Norwood and Addington. In a conservation area, trees are protected even without a TPO. You must give the council six weeks' written notice before cutting down or pruning a tree with a trunk over 75mm in diameter, measured 1.5m above ground. We prepare and submit the notice for you. See our conservation area guide.

Dead, dying or dangerous trees

There are exemptions, but they're narrower than most people think. Dead or immediately dangerous trees can often be dealt with, but in a conservation area you still need to give five working days' notice first, and you should keep photographic evidence. Don't assume. Ask us.

Nesting birds and bats

It's illegal to disturb nesting birds, typically from March to August, or to harm bat roosts. This can affect timing even when the tree has no other protection. See our nesting season guide.

Council-owned and neighbours' trees

Street and park trees belong to the council, so you can't touch them. Report problems directly to Croydon Council. If a neighbour's tree overhangs your garden, you can cut back branches to the boundary, but you must offer the cuttings back and you can't harm the tree. If you're in a conservation area or the tree has a TPO, the rules above still apply. For problem evergreen hedges over 2m tall, the High Hedges complaint route through the council may apply.

Planning conditions

Trees on newer developments are often protected by planning conditions, even with no TPO, so check before you cut. See Do I need permission to fell a tree?

Tree Surgeon Croydon: Frequently Asked Questions

Costs and Quotes

How much does a tree surgeon cost in Croydon?

Most domestic tree work in Croydon costs between £200 and £1,100, with large trees at £1,000 to £3,000+, depending on the job. Pruning a small tree is at the lower end, while removing a large tree in a tight garden or near buildings is at the higher end. Our free quote gives you a fixed price, and our cost calculator gives an instant estimate.

How much does tree removal cost in Croydon?

Tree removal in Croydon typically costs £250 to £3,000+. The price depends on tree height and spread, access, whether it must be dismantled in sections, proximity to buildings or power lines, and whether the waste and stump are removed. A clear written quote shows exactly what's included.

How much does stump grinding cost in Croydon?

Stump grinding in Croydon usually costs £120 to £700 per stump, based on diameter, root spread and access for the machine. Multiple stumps in one visit are cheaper per stump.

What affects the price of tree work?

The main factors are tree size and species, access to the garden, the risk to nearby property, whether a crane or rigging is needed, waste removal, and any council notice or consent we need to arrange. Emergency and out-of-hours work costs more.

Is the quote really free with no obligation?

Yes. We visit, assess the tree and the legal position, and give you a written quote. You're under no obligation to book.

Council Rules, TPOs and Conservation Areas

Do I need permission to remove a tree in Croydon?

It depends on the tree. You need written consent from Croydon Council if it has a Tree Preservation Order (TPO), and you must give six weeks' written notice before work on most trees in a conservation area. Unprotected trees in a garden outside a conservation area generally need no permission, but always check first.

How do I know if my tree has a TPO?

Ask Croydon Council's planning or tree team, or send us your address and a photo. We check TPOs and conservation area status for you before we quote, at no extra cost.

How do I know if I live in a conservation area in Croydon?

Check Croydon Council's conservation area map, or send us your postcode. Parts of Purley, South Croydon, Upper Norwood and Addington are covered, but the boundaries are specific and can cover one side of a road and not the other.

What happens if I cut down a protected tree without permission?

It's a criminal offence. The fine can be up to £20,000 in the magistrates' court, and more if the case goes to the Crown Court, and you may be required to plant a replacement tree. Always get the legal position confirmed in writing before work starts.

Can I remove a dead or dangerous tree without permission?

Often yes, but there are conditions. For a TPO tree, exemptions for dead or immediately dangerous trees exist, but you should keep evidence. In a conservation area you must still give five working days' notice, and for TPO trees the council may need to be informed. If in doubt, ask us first.

Emergency and Dangerous Trees

Do you offer emergency tree surgeon services in Croydon?

Yes. We handle storm-damaged, fallen and dangerous trees, including trees on fences, sheds, cars and roofs. Call 07881 305352 and we'll tell you how quickly we can attend and make the area safe. See emergency tree work.

What should I do if a tree has fallen on my house or car?

Keep everyone away from the tree and the damaged area, and call 999 if there's immediate danger to life. If the tree has brought down power lines, stay well clear and call 105 to report it. Then call us to make the tree safe and clear it. Photograph the damage for your insurer.

Who is responsible for a fallen tree: me or the council?

The tree's owner is responsible. If it's on private land, that's usually the landowner. Street and park trees belong to Croydon Council, so report those to the council. If a neighbour's tree falls on your property, tell your insurer and the neighbour, and take photos before any clearance.

Specific Tree Jobs

Can you remove a tree that is overhanging or close to my house?

Yes. We specialise in controlled removal and reduction of trees over houses, conservatories, garages and power lines using sectional dismantling and rigging, so nothing lands on your property. Photos help us quote quickly.

Can tree roots damage my foundations or drains?

They can, especially in clay soils, and Croydon has areas with shrinkable clay. Look for cracks, sticking doors or blocked drains, and contact your insurer or a structural engineer. Never remove a large tree near a building without advice, because it can sometimes make things worse.

Do I need to grind the stump out after tree removal?

Not always, but we recommend it if you want to lawn, patio or replant, or if the stump is a trip hazard or is regrowing. Leaving it is fine for a wildlife stump or a seat.

How much can I cut back a neighbour's overhanging branches?

You can cut branches back to the boundary line, but no further and not in a way that damages the tree. The branches technically belong to the tree owner, so offer the cuttings back. TPO and conservation area rules still apply.

What can I do about a neighbour's tall leylandii hedge?

Speak to your neighbour first. If that fails, the High Hedges complaint process through the council may apply to evergreen hedges more than 2 metres tall that block light. We can reduce or remove hedges on your own property.

Practical Questions

Are you insured and qualified?

Yes. We carry £5 million public liability insurance and our operators hold NPTC/LANTRA certificates for chainsaw use and tree climbing. You're welcome to see the documents before we start.

What qualifications should a tree surgeon have?

At minimum, certificates in chainsaw operation and tree climbing and aerial rescue, from NPTC/LANTRA or equivalent, plus public liability insurance. Ask to see both. Avoid anyone who can't show them or who only offers a cash price. See how to choose a tree surgeon.

Do you take away the waste?

Yes. Branches are chipped and removed, and logs can be left or taken away as you prefer. Please tell us which when you book.

How long does tree work take?

Most domestic jobs take between a few hours and a day. Large or complex removals may take longer. We confirm the timing when we quote.

When is the best time of year for tree work?

Most trees can be worked on year-round. The main constraints are nesting season (March to August), when we check for birds first, and species-specific timing, for example pruning cherry and plum in summer. We'll advise on the right timing for your tree.

Which areas of Croydon do you cover?

We cover all of Croydon and nearby areas, including South Croydon, Purley, Sanderstead, Selsdon, Addington, Shirley, Coulsdon, Kenley, Thornton Heath, Norbury, Crystal Palace and Upper Norwood, across CR0, CR2, CR5, CR7, CR8, SE19 and SW16. See our area pages.

Can I rent a property and still get a tree removed?

You'll need the landlord's or freeholder's permission, since the tree belongs to the property owner. We can survey the tree and provide a report or quote for the landlord or agent.
