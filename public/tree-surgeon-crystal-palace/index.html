Tree Surgeon in Crystal Palace and Upper Norwood, SE19

Upper Norwood sits on the Norwood Ridge, one of the highest parts of south London, and it is packed with big Victorian villas, steep gardens and mature trees. It is also covered by several conservation areas, and the borough boundary runs down the middle of some of its main roads. So the first questions are always the same: whose tree is it, which council covers it, and what does that council need before we cut? We do tree surgery on the Croydon side, and we answer those questions before we quote.

Which borough is your tree in?

Crystal Palace and Upper Norwood meet several boroughs. The council's appraisal of the Upper Norwood Triangle says its northern edge along Westow Hill abuts Lambeth, its south-eastern edge along Church Road abuts Bromley, and the boundary runs down the middle of both roads.

That means the house on one side of the road can be under Croydon's tree rules and the house opposite under a different council's. We work in the London Borough of Croydon. Give us your full address and we confirm the borough before we quote. If you are on the other side, the rules, the notice period and the contact details will differ.

What the rules mean for your tree

  • Prior approval. The council's appraisals for Harold Road, Beulah Hill and Church Road all say most work to trees in the conservation area needs the council's prior approval. In practice that means a written notice six weeks before the work.
  • TPOs on top. Church Road and Beulah Hill have many TPOs, so a tree can be protected twice over.
  • Penalties. Cutting without consent can bring a fine running to five figures.
  • What the council wants to keep. The appraisals say front-garden trees should be kept and extended, and they list loss of trees as a threat. Expect a question about replacement.
  • No blanket extra restriction. The Harold Road and Beulah Hill appraisals state that no Article 4 Direction applies to them, which concerns buildings and boundaries, not trees.

We prepare the notice, with photographs, the work described plainly and the reason. See our guides to conservation area tree work and tree preservation orders.

If your garden backs onto a park or wood, assume the land beyond the fence is the council's, and trim only what hangs over your side. Ash trees are among the species recorded here, so see ash dieback in Croydon if yours has a thinning crown.

Hillside gardens and awkward access

Upper Norwood sits at about 110 m above sea level on the Norwood Ridge, and the ground falls away sharply from it. For tree work that means:

  • Slopes and steps. Many gardens drop steeply from the house. Rigging and lowering pieces takes longer, and bringing the machine to the tree is harder.
  • Large trees in villa gardens. Villas on 13 to 20 m plots, or bigger in places, grow trees that need rigging over roofs and neighbouring plots. See tree removal and tree pruning.
  • Narrow lanes and terraces. Fox Hill and Grange Hill have narrow plots, and some streets in the Triangle have little or no pavement. We check the route before we book.
  • Parking. A van or chipper on controlled roads may need a council bay suspension. We book it with at least 10 working days' notice.

Clay, roots and buildings on the ridge

The council's appraisals describe the Norwood Ridge as London Clay capped in places by Claygate Beds gravel. On clay, big thirsty trees close to older houses can matter more than on chalk or sand. See trees near buildings for how that works, and why you should talk to your insurer before cutting a tree that might be involved in cracking. A tree survey can record the tree's size, species and condition.

Tree Surgeon in Crystal Palace and Upper Norwood, SE19: frequently asked questions

Is Upper Norwood a conservation area?

Large parts of it are. Croydon records the Upper Norwood Triangle, Church Road, Harold Road and Beulah Hill as designated. Check your address on the council's map.

Is Harold Road a conservation area?

Yes. Croydon's own pages record it as designated in April 1973, with extensions in 1992, 2008 and 2015.

Which council do I deal with for a tree in Crystal Palace?

The one your property is in. The boundary runs down the middle of Westow Hill and Church Road, so tell us your full address.

Do I need permission to cut a tree in Upper Norwood?

In a conservation area, most work needs the council's prior approval, given through a notice that goes in six weeks ahead. It costs nothing, and we prepare it.

What if my tree is protected by a TPO as well?

Then you need the council's consent too. We check both before we quote.

Can you work on a steep garden?

Yes. We plan the route, rigging and drop zone around the slope. Photos from the top and bottom help.

My neighbour's tree is overhanging. What can I do?

Talk to them first. Trimming back to the fence line is usually allowed, but conservation area notice still applies.

What will tree work cost on a hillside plot?

From around £200 for small jobs. A large tree on a slope or over a building can pass £3,900.

Do you cover SE19 and Crystal Palace?

We cover the Croydon side of SE19. If your address might be in another borough, ask us.

What if a tree comes down in a storm?

Ring us in working hours, and use the safety numbers on our emergency page if there is danger at other times.
