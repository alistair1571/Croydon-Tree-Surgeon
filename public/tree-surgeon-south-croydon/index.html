The Croham Manor Road Conservation Area

Croydon Council designated the Croham Manor Road Conservation Area in October 2008. It sits on the lower slopes of Croham Hurst, bounded to the south by Selsdon Road and partly to the east by the Hurst, and its main streets are Croham Manor Road and Hurst Way.

The council's appraisal describes it as:

  • 22 separate plots, all detached single-family houses.
  • Mainly 1920s and 1930s Mock-Tudor and Arts and Crafts style, with a handful of Edwardian houses.
  • Large houses behind high boundaries and mature planting, with the oldest, at 53 Croham Manor Road, dated 1909.
  • About 90 to 100 m above sea level, on a slope that falls from east to west, so some gardens drop away in front of the house.

What that means for trees. The appraisal says trees in the conservation area are protected, and points to the council's general rules, which mean written notice six weeks before most pruning or felling. It also says mature trees and planting in front gardens have a significant positive impact on the area, so the council is likely to ask what replaces anything you remove. We prepare the notice and the fine print is in the guide below. See conservation area tree work.

Elsewhere in South Croydon, most roads are not in a conservation area. A TPO can still protect an individual tree, and we check that first. See tree preservation orders. Check the council's map for your exact road, because it has said it is reviewing further areas for possible designation, and boundaries change.

Street trees such as the whitebeam and planes are normally the council's, so report problems rather than pruning them yourself. Trees inside your boundary are yours, and in the conservation area they are protected.

Living beside Croham Hurst

Croham Hurst is a council-run site of about 34 hectares: ancient oak woodland and heath, a Site of Special Scientific Interest, and home to a scheduled Bronze Age round barrow. Its draft management plan describes high forest dominated by oak, with beech, holly, birch, ash, field maple and Scots pine, and few houses back directly onto it.

If your garden does border the Hurst:

  • The woodland is not yours to cut or clear, and it is an SSSI, so damage to it is a serious matter. Contact the council's parks team about anything on its side.
  • Anything that falls from it is a liability question, not automatically the council's. Our storm damage guide explains who pays, and a photograph on the day helps.
  • Your own large trees near the boundary can matter as much as the woodland. A tall oak or pine in your garden is yours to manage, within the conservation area rules where they apply.
  • Birds and wildlife. The Hurst's own management plan times works for August to February to avoid disturbing breeding birds. We take the same care in gardens beside it. See tree work in nesting season.

Slopes, long drives and big gardens

Large plots make access easier in some ways: long drives, space to park off the road, and room to drop sections. They make it harder in others.

  • Falling ground. Where the garden drops away, rigging, ropes and the route for equipment all take longer.
  • Tall hedges and boundaries. Properties here often sit behind high hedges, which are part of the area's character. See hedge cutting.
  • Big trees over big roofs. Large specimens need careful sectional removal or reduction. See tree removal and tree pruning.
  • Roots and older buildings. Chalk and sand are usually safer for foundations than clay, but you should check your own ground. See trees near buildings.
  • A survey before you buy or build. See tree surveys.

Close to the border: The Waldrons

The Waldrons Conservation Area, designated in 1973, lies in Waddon ward about a mile south-east of the town centre, around Southbridge Road, Bramley Hill and Duppas Hill. It is a separate area with its own appraisal, and numbers 5 to 23 The Waldrons are covered by an Article 4 Direction on hard surfaces and boundaries. If your address is near there, ask us and we will confirm which area applies.

Parking and access

Long drives mean most jobs here park on site. If a lorry or platform has to stand in the road, the council needs about two weeks' warning for a suspension.

Tree Surgeon in South Croydon, CR2: frequently asked questions

Is South Croydon a conservation area?

Parts are. The Croham Manor Road Conservation Area, designated in October 2008, covers Croham Manor Road and Hurst Way. Most other South Croydon roads are not, but check the council's map.

Do I need permission to cut a tree on Hurst Way?

Yes, for most work. You notify Croydon Council six weeks beforehand, at no cost, and we draft it.

What about a tree outside the conservation area?

It could still carry a TPO, so we check before we quote.

Who owns Croham Hurst?

Croydon Council manages it. It is a Site of Special Scientific Interest, so don't cut or clear anything on its land.

A branch from the Hurst has come down in my garden. What do I do?

Take photos, tell your insurer and the council, and don't assume you are liable.

Can you work on a steeply sloping garden?

Yes. We plan the route and the rigging around the slope.

What does removing a big tree cost in South Croydon?

A medium-large tree runs £550 to £1,100, and a large one £1,000 to £3,000 or more, depending on access.

Do you work in all of CR2?

Yes, South Croydon is part of our borough patch.

What about storm damage?

Phone us in working hours, and use the numbers on our emergency page if there is danger out of hours.
