Is your tree protected? Restrictions in South Croydon

The Croham Manor Road Conservation Area, beside Croham Hurst, was designated in October 2008, and Croydon Council's appraisal states that trees in conservation areas are protected. The council says you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area, with fines of up to £20,000 for cutting one down without consent and up to £2,500 for pruning one. The appraisal We read dates from 2012 to 2013, so check the current boundary on the council's map.

Croydon Council has also named Croham Park Estate among nine areas it would consider for conservation area status; check where it sits and whether it has been designated. Croham Hurst is described as ancient woodland on the council's local list of historic parks and gardens, and contains a scheduled Bronze Age round barrow. Report trees on council land to the council. See the conservation area guide.

Access, parking and neighbours

The appraisal notes ample off-street parking and, on part of the road, long shared driveways; the land falls away in places, which affects how machinery reaches a tree. Slope and gaps are assessed before the visit.

If a vehicle or chipper needs road space, Croydon Council offers a dispensation to park on yellow lines while work is carried out, or a suspension of a parking bay. It asks for at least five working days' notice, payment first and a separate licence for a skip on the road. Check current fees on the council's page.

Tell us if a tree is on a boundary or overhangs next door. You may be able to cut branches back to the boundary but not beyond it, and cuttings should be offered back. Work only goes ahead with the tree owner's consent.
