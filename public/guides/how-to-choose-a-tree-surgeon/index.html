How to Choose a Tree Surgeon in Croydon

Tree work is one of the most dangerous jobs in the UK, and the trade is not directly regulated, so anyone can put a van on the road and call themselves a tree surgeon. The wrong choice can mean a damaged tree, a damaged roof, an uninsured accident on your property, or a fine of up to £20,000 if the tree was protected. This guide is the checklist we would use ourselves: who to hire for which job, the certificates and insurance to ask for, what a proper quote contains, the warning signs, and fifteen questions to put to anyone. The short answers

  • Qualified? Look for current chainsaw and climbing certificates (NPTC or LANTRA, or equivalent). Qualifications.
  • Insured? Public liability, plus employers' liability if they have staff. Insurance.
  • How many quotes? Two or three for anything beyond a small job. Quotes.
  • Biggest red flag? A cold caller offering to start today. Red flags.
  • Must they check for a TPO? Yes, before quoting. Local checks.

Who should you actually hire?

Different problems need different people:

  • Pruning, removal and making a tree safe: a tree surgeon (arborist).
  • An independent health or risk report, or a planning survey: an arboricultural consultant. Councils generally expect a suitably qualified specialist for planning surveys. See tree surveys.
  • The cause of cracks in a wall: a structural engineer. Do not remove the tree first. See trees near buildings.
  • Low garden hedges: a gardener or hedge specialist. See hedge cutting.
  • Large felling: not a general gardener or handyman.

Check qualifications

  • Certificates. Staff on site should hold chainsaw certificates (including felling), and tree climbing and aerial rescue certificates, usually NPTC or LANTRA. Coventry City Council's guidance says competent arborists carry an A4 certificate or a plastic ID card listing the units of competence passed.
  • Broader training. National certificates and diplomas in arboriculture show more than the minimum.
  • Match certificates to people. Ask who is doing the work, and check certificates for them, including any subcontractors.
  • Check they are current. Ask to see copies. An advert or a van sign guarantees nothing.
  • Accreditation. The Arboricultural Association's ARB Approved Contractor scheme assesses firms on health and safety, customer care, site inspections and work quality. Check the Association's directory yourself, because some firms falsely claim approval. The ISA Certified Arborist scheme certifies individuals, and you can verify it on the ISA website.
  • Memberships. They show commitment but do not guarantee standards.

Check insurance and what it covers

  • Public liability covers damage to property or injury caused by the work. The Arboricultural Association and Coventry City Council recommend at least £5 million. Some councils set a lower floor (Windsor and Maidenhead asks for £2 million, rising to £10 million beside the public highway), so £5 million is a sensible minimum for a house in a typical street.
  • Employers' liability is legally required for firms with employees, but not for a genuine sole trader working alone.
  • Professional indemnity matters if you are paying for a survey or report.
  • Check the certificate itself. Look at the business name, the dates and that tree work is covered. A photo is not proof, so phone the insurer if you are unsure.
  • Uninsured work can leave you liable for neighbours' property damage.

They should ask about TPOs and conservation areas

A good tree surgeon checks for Tree Preservation Orders and conservation areas before quoting, and checks for nesting birds and bats before starting. If they do not ask, assume they will not check. Felling a protected tree without permission can cost up to £20,000, and the person doing the work can be prosecuted as well as you. See tree preservation orders, conservation area tree work and the nesting season guide.

Fifteen questions to ask

  1. Can I see your certificates and the insurance certificate?
  2. Who will actually do the work?
  3. Do you check for TPOs and conservation areas, and who applies?
  4. Do you work to BS 3998, the British Standard for tree work?
  5. What exactly will you do to each tree?
  6. Will you top any tree? (The right answer is no. See below.)
  7. Is waste removal included? Do I keep the logs?
  8. What happens to the stump?
  9. How will you protect my lawn, roof, fence and neighbours' property?
  10. How will you check for nesting birds and bats?
  11. Is the price fixed, and does it include VAT?
  12. When will you start, and how long will it take?
  13. What are the payment terms? Deposits are uncommon for routine work, so a large upfront cash demand is a concern.
  14. Are you a registered waste carrier?
  15. Can you give me a reference, or photos of similar work?

What a good written quote looks like

  • The business name, address and a landline or phone number.
  • Work described tree by tree, with a reference to BS 3998.
  • How waste will be handled, and whether it is included.
  • Stump treatment and height.
  • The total, and whether VAT is included.
  • Who handles TPO or conservation area permissions, and any extra fee.
  • Insurance details, dates and duration.
  • Payment terms.

Get two or three quotes for anything beyond a small job, arrange site visits (normally free) and explain what you want to achieve, not only the task. Accept your chosen quote in writing and tell the others why they were not chosen. See tree removal cost.

Is a cheap tree surgeon worth it?

Rarely. A price far below the others usually leaves something out: insurance, qualified staff, waste removal, protection checks, the right method or wildlife checks. Do not choose on price alone. Reliability, courtesy and safe, good practice matter more.

Why "topping" is a red flag

Topping means cutting branches back to stubs. It harms the tree, produces weak, fast regrowth and shortens its life. Proper crown reduction removes branches back to suitable growth points, to a planned amount. A surgeon who offers to top a tree is either unqualified or cutting corners. See tree pruning.

Red flags: walk away if you see these

  • Cold callers and door-knockers, especially after storms. The Arboricultural Association says firms that post leaflets or knock on your door are usually best avoided.
  • Cash only, with no written quote, receipt or insurance.
  • Refusing to show certificates or insurance.
  • No questions about TPOs, conservation areas or wildlife.
  • Offers to top or "lop" the tree.
  • Pressure to decide now, or a big upfront payment.
  • A vague description such as "tidy up".
  • No fixed address or phone number.
  • Unmarked waste vehicles.
  • Climbers without helmets, eye and hearing protection, chainsaw-resistant clothing or a harness, or ground crew with no hi-vis or traffic management when working beside a road.

Doorstep sales. You can usually cancel a doorstep contract within 14 days, and the trader must tell you so in writing. Report problem traders to Trading Standards or through Citizens Advice.

Check waste carrier registration

Tree waste is controlled waste. Anyone carrying it commercially in England must be registered, and you can check the register on the Environment Agency site or by phoning 03708 506506. Fly-tipped waste can be traced back to the householder who hired the firm.

Reading reviews and references

  • Look for steady reviews over time with specific details, local jobs and photos.
  • Be wary of bursts of five-star reviews, near-identical wording or reviews that describe no real work.
  • Ask for a reference and check an owner's replies to complaints.

Choosing a tree surgeon in Croydon: local questions

  • Which council applies? Near Norbury and Crystal Palace, it depends on your exact address. See Norbury and Crystal Palace.
  • Can they work in a narrow terrace garden with no vehicle access? Common in Addiscombe, Thornton Heath and Norbury.
  • Can they handle steep, wooded or hillside gardens? Common in Purley, Kenley, Coulsdon and Sanderstead.
  • Will they check for bats and nests near commons and woodland? Important in Shirley, Selsdon and the south.
  • Are you a tenant or leaseholder? Your landlord, agent or freeholder may need to agree first. See do I need permission to fell a tree.

Special situations

  • Emergency or storm work. Still confirm insurance, certificates and a price before starting. See storm-damaged trees.
  • A tree with cracks or subsidence nearby. Speak to your insurer and a structural engineer before anyone cuts it.
  • Ash dieback. Brittle trees need experience and insurance. See ash dieback in Croydon.
  • Conifer hedges. A good hedge cutter says honestly if a conifer cannot be cut back hard, and checks for nests first.

How to choose a tree surgeon: frequently asked questions

How do I find a good tree surgeon?

Use the Arboricultural Association's ARB Approved Contractor directory and recommendations, get two or three written quotes and check certificates and insurance.

What qualifications should a tree surgeon have?

Current chainsaw and climbing certificates, typically NPTC or LANTRA, matching the people on site.

How much insurance should a tree surgeon have?

The Arboricultural Association recommends at least £5 million public liability, plus employers' liability if they have staff.

Do tree surgeons need a licence?

Tree work is not directly regulated, so check certificates, insurance and accreditation yourself.

What is BS 3998?

The British Standard for tree work. A good contractor works to it and says so in the quote.

What is tree topping and why avoid it?

Cutting branches back to stubs. It harms the tree and produces weak regrowth, so crown reduction is preferred.

Should I pay a deposit?

Deposits are uncommon for routine work. Be wary of large upfront cash demands.

Do I need to check a tree surgeon is a registered waste carrier?

Yes. Check the register or phone 03708 506506.

Who checks whether my tree has a TPO?

A good surgeon checks before quoting. You can also email development.management@croydon.gov.uk.

What if the work goes wrong?

Raise it in writing, then contact Citizens Advice, Trading Standards or your insurer.

Is the cheapest quote the best?

Rarely. Compare line by line for insurance, waste and method.

Can I ask you these questions?

Yes. Ask us anything on this page before you book, and ask for the answers in writing.
