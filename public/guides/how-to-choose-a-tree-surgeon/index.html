How to Choose a Tree Surgeon in Croydon

A good tree surgeon is qualified, properly insured, checks whether your tree is protected before quoting, gives you a written, itemised price, and is happy to be asked questions. A bad one can leave you with a damaged tree, a damaged house, a fine of up to £20,000, or no insurance cover when something goes wrong. This guide gives you the checks that matter, the questions to ask and the red flags to walk away from.

Check Qualifications

Tree work is one of the most dangerous jobs in the UK, so training matters, not just for the quality of the cut but for the safety of your property and neighbours.

QualificationWhat it shows
Chainsaw certificates (including felling)The operator is trained to use a chainsaw safely
Tree climbing and aerial rescueThe climber can work at height and rescue a colleague
Professional tree inspectionThey can assess trees for defects and risk
Level 2 or 3 diplomas in arboriculture or forestryBroader training in tree care
Professional membershipsFor example the Arboricultural Association, which has an approved contractor scheme

How to check: ask to see the certificates and check the names match the people who'll be on site; check they're current; ask who will actually do the work, including any subcontractors.

Check Insurance, and What It Covers

Insurance is the single biggest protection you have. If a tree surgeon drops a branch on your conservatory or your neighbour's car, their insurance should pay, not you.

InsuranceWhat it coversWhat to check
Public liabilityDamage to your property, your neighbours' property, or injury to people caused by the workThat it covers tree surgery specifically, including work at height and felling near buildings, and the amount of cover. We carry £5 million
Employers' liabilityInjury to staffLegally required if they employ people. It doesn't apply to a genuine sole trader working alone
Professional indemnityAdvice and reportsRelevant if they provide surveys or reports

Ask for the certificate and check the name matches the business, the dates are current and the activities include tree work. Ask about subcontractors, and check for exclusions. A photo of a certificate isn't proof, so if in doubt, call the insurer. A cheap uninsured quote is rarely cheap, because you could be liable for damage to a neighbour's property.

They Should Ask About TPOs and Conservation Areas

This is the clearest test of a good tree surgeon in Croydon, because so many of our trees are protected. Before quoting, a good one will ask for your address, check whether the tree has a TPO or is in a conservation area, tell you what permission you need and who prepares it, and check for nesting birds and bats.

Cutting down a protected tree without permission can lead to a fine of up to £20,000, and the person who carries out the work can be prosecuted, not just the owner. If a tree surgeon offers to cut your tree without asking about any of this, that's a warning sign. See our TPO guide, conservation area guide and Do I need permission to fell a tree?

Is a Cheap Tree Surgeon Worth It?

A low price isn't automatically a bad sign, but a price far below everyone else's usually means something's missing.

What a very low price might leave outThe risk to you
InsuranceIf something goes wrong, you pay
Qualified staffHigher risk of accidents and poor work
Waste removalThe mess left behind, or fly-tipping traced back to you
Checks for protected treesA fine, which could be far more than the saving
Proper methodTopping or poor pruning that harms the tree
Wildlife checksPossible offences

Cheap isn't the same as good value. A fair price pays for a trained team, insurance, safe equipment, legal waste disposal and the paperwork.

Red Flags: Walk Away If You See These

  • Cold callers at your door offering to cut your tree today, especially after a storm
  • Cash only, with no written quote, receipt or insurance
  • Won't show insurance or certificates, or makes excuses
  • No questions about TPOs or conservation areas
  • Offers to "top" the tree. It damages the tree, and isn't good practice
  • Pressure to decide on the spot, or "price only valid today"
  • A large upfront payment
  • Vague descriptions, such as "tidy up the tree"
  • No fixed address or phone number, only a mobile and a first name
  • An unmarked waste vehicle, which may mean illegal dumping that can be traced back to you

If you've been approached at the door: you generally have a right to cancel contracts made in your home or away from the trader's premises within 14 days, and the trader must tell you so in writing. Don't agree to a same-day start to "get it done", and take advice before paying. Report doorstep traders to Trading Standards or through the Citizens Advice consumer service.

Check Waste Carrier Registration

Tree waste is controlled waste. A business carrying it commercially in England needs to be registered as a waste carrier, and you can check the register on GOV.UK. Fly-tipped garden waste can be traced back to the householder who hired the firm. Ask for the registration number and check it.

Special Situations

Emergency work. After a storm, people choose in a hurry, which is exactly when rogue traders appear. Even in an emergency, ask: Are you insured? Can I see your certificate? What's the price before you start? A good firm gives straight answers even at short notice. See emergency tree work.

Cracks or subsidence. Don't hire anyone to remove the tree before taking advice from your insurer and a structural engineer. See trees near buildings.

Ash dieback. Trees with ash dieback can be brittle and dangerous, so check experience and insurance carefully. See ash dieback.

Hedges and leylandii. A good hedge cutter tells you honestly if a conifer hedge can't be cut back hard without leaving bare brown wood, and checks for nests first. See hedge cutting.

How to Choose a Tree Surgeon: FAQs

How do I choose a good tree surgeon?

Check their qualifications and insurance, make sure they ask about TPOs and conservation areas, get a written, itemised quote, and look for genuine local reviews. Walk away from cold callers, cash-only deals and anyone offering to top your tree.

What qualifications should a tree surgeon have?

At minimum, certificates in chainsaw operation and in tree climbing and aerial rescue, from NPTC/LANTRA or an equivalent body. Ask to see them.

What insurance should a tree surgeon have?

Public liability insurance that covers tree work, including work at height and felling near buildings, and employers' liability if they employ staff. Ask for the certificate and check it's current.

What happens if an uninsured tree surgeon damages my property?

You may have to pay yourself, and you could be liable for damage to a neighbour's property.

Should a tree surgeon ask about TPOs?

Yes. A good one checks for TPOs and conservation areas before quoting and explains what permission you need. If they don't ask, be careful.

What is the best way to compare tree surgeon quotes?

Get two or three written quotes, and compare what's included (waste, stump, VAT, insurance) as well as the price.

Why is one tree surgeon so much cheaper than another?

Check what the quote leaves out. A very low price may omit insurance, qualified staff, waste removal or protection checks, or involve unsafe practices such as topping.

Is it OK to pay a tree surgeon in cash?

It can be, but insist on a written quote and a receipt. Be wary of anyone who will only work for cash with no paperwork.

What should I do if a tree surgeon knocks on my door?

Don't agree to work there and then. Ask for their details, and use a firm you've checked. You may have a right to cancel within 14 days, but it's safer not to agree at all.

What is "topping" and why is it bad?

Cutting a tree's branches back to stubs. It can harm the tree and produce weak regrowth, so a good tree surgeon uses crown reduction instead.

What is BS 3998?

The British Standard for tree work. A good tree surgeon works to it, or can explain their method.

Do I need an arboricultural consultant or a tree surgeon?

A tree surgeon does the work, and a consultant gives independent advice and reports. Many jobs need only a tree surgeon, but planning surveys and some disputes need a consultant.

What if I'm not happy with the work?

Tell them promptly, in writing, and give them the chance to put it right. If it isn't resolved, contact Citizens Advice, Trading Standards or your insurer.

Do you offer a free quote?

Yes. Site visits and written quotes are free, with no obligation.
