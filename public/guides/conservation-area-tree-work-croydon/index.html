Tree Work in a Croydon Conservation Area

If your tree is in a conservation area, you normally must give Croydon Council six weeks' written notice before cutting it down or pruning it, even if it has no TPO. This guide explains which trees are covered, how to tell, how the notice works, and what happens next.

The Rule

Croydon Council says you must give the council six weeks' written notice before carrying out work to trees in a conservation area. It says a fine of up to £20,000 can be imposed for destroying or cutting down a tree there without consent, and up to £2,500 for pruning one without consent. The relevant law is section 211 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990.

Which Trees Are Covered?

Nationally, the notice requirement applies to trees with a trunk diameter of more than 75mm, measured 1.5 metres above ground level (100mm if you are thinning to help other trees grow). Croydon's page doesn't mention a small-tree exemption, so do not assume one applies, and check with the council or us before treating a tree as too small to count.

You won't usually have a diameter tape. A tape measure around the trunk gives you the circumference:

Trunk diameterCircumference at 1.5m (tape measure around the trunk)
75mmabout 236mm (about 9.3 inches)
100mmabout 314mm (about 12.4 inches)

Measure around the trunk at 1.5m above the ground (roughly chest height). If it's more than about 236mm round, treat it as covered. Where a tree has several stems, measure each one. The rule covers trees, not ordinary garden hedges or small shrubs, but trees growing in a hedge can be covered. See hedge cutting.

Where the Conservation Areas Are

Our area pages cover those we have checked, including the Croham Manor Road, East India Estate, Norbury Estate, Webb Estate and Upper Woodcote Village and Kenley Aerodrome conservation areas, and those in Upper Norwood. Croydon has a number of conservation areas. Boundaries change, and they can cover one side of a road and not the other, so check the council's current map.

How to check whether you're in one:

  1. Use Croydon Council's conservation area map.
  2. Ask the planning team, with your address.
  3. Look at your property papers: designation is normally recorded as a local land charge.
  4. Ask us. Send an address and we'll check as part of every quote.

In areas near borough boundaries, such as Crystal Palace and Upper Norwood and Norbury, the council that applies depends on the exact address.

Areas the Council Is Considering

Croydon Council has named nine areas it would consider for conservation area status, including Croham Park Estate, Court Avenue in Coulsdon, Sanderstead Village and the Whitgift Estate, and its Cabinet agreed a list of potential new areas in December 2025. Until an area is designated the rule does not apply to it, but it's worth knowing if you're planning tree work in one of them. See Coulsdon, Sanderstead and Shirley.

How the Six-Week Notice Works

StepWhat happens
1. You send a noticeWritten notice to Croydon Council, with the address, species, exact work, reasons and photographs. There's no council fee.
2. The council registers itThe six weeks run from when it receives a valid notice
3. The council considers itIt can make a TPO, or have no objection
4. Outcome A: a TPO is madeThe tree is now protected, and you'll need to apply for consent in the usual way
5. Outcome B: no objection or no responseYou can carry out the work as described once the six weeks have passed, or sooner if the council confirms it has no objection
6. Carry out the workExactly as described in the notice, normally within two years of the notice.

Don't start early unless the council has told you in writing it has no objection.

Emergencies

Other councils say there is an emergency exemption for dead or dangerous trees, but Croydon's page does not say so. Do not rely on one without checking with the council. If a tree in a conservation area is an immediate danger, limit the work to what is needed to remove the danger, photograph the tree before you start, tell the council as soon as you can, and keep records. See emergency tree work.

Practical Tips

  • Photograph the tree from several angles before you send the notice, and keep copies.
  • Be specific about the work. "Reduce by up to 2m" is clearer than "prune".
  • Plan around nesting season. Active nests are protected, and a notice period doesn't override wildlife law. See our nesting season guide.
  • Don't wait for an emergency. If you know a tree needs work, give notice early.
  • Keep the paperwork: the notice, the council's reply and the date.
  • For a neighbour's tree, the owner gives the notice, and you need their agreement.
  • Subsidence: if a tree may be affecting your building, take advice from your insurer and engineer before removing it. See trees near buildings.

Conservation Area FAQs

Do I need permission to prune a tree in a conservation area?

You need to give the council six weeks' written notice, not permission as such, before pruning a tree in a Croydon conservation area.

What size of tree is covered?

Nationally, a trunk diameter over 75mm at 1.5m above ground (100mm if thinning to help other trees). That's about 236mm round the trunk. Croydon's page doesn't mention an exemption, so check before assuming a tree is too small.

How do I measure my tree?

Wrap a tape measure around the trunk at 1.5m above ground. If it's more than about 236mm, treat it as covered. Ask us if the tree has several stems.

How long does the notice take?

Six weeks, unless the council confirms in writing that it has no objection sooner.

What happens if the council makes a TPO?

The tree becomes protected, and you'd need to apply for consent for the work in the usual way.

Does it cost anything to give notice?

There's no council fee for a conservation area notice.

Can I remove a dead tree in a conservation area?

Give the council notice first and keep photographic evidence. A dying tree isn't dead. Croydon's page doesn't mention an emergency exemption, so check with the council.

Does it apply to hedges?

Not to a garden hedge itself, but trees growing in a hedge can be covered.

What are the penalties?

Croydon Council says up to £20,000 for cutting down a tree in a conservation area without consent and up to £2,500 for pruning one. You may also have to replant.

What if my tree has a TPO and is in a conservation area?

The TPO rules apply, so you apply for consent rather than giving notice.

Which council do I deal with near a borough boundary?

It depends on your exact address. Tell us your address and we'll check.
