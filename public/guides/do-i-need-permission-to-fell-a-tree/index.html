Do I Need Permission to Fell a Tree in Croydon?

Often not, but sometimes yes, and getting it wrong can mean a fine of up to £20,000. In England you generally don't need permission to cut down a tree in your own garden, unless it's protected or something else stops you. This page gives you a 60-second check, then tells you where to go for the detail.

The 60-Second Check

Answer these in order. Stop at the first "Yes".

#QuestionIf yes
1Is the tree yours? The trunk stands on your land, and you own or manage the propertyIf No, you can't fell it. See "Common situations" below
2Does the tree have a TPO?Cutting down a protected tree without permission can bring a fine of up to £20,000, according to Croydon Council. See our TPO guide
3Is it in a conservation area?You must give six weeks' written notice first. See our conservation area guide
4Is it dead, dying or dangerous?Different rules apply, and only urgent necessary works can be done on a dangerous protected tree without consent
5Are birds nesting or bats roosting?Active nests and bats are protected. See our nesting season guide
6Is there a planning condition on the tree? Common on newer estates and after developmentYou may need the council's agreement. Check your planning papers

8Is it near overhead cables, a railway or the highway?Special rules and contacts apply. Don't work near them yourself
9None of the above?You can generally go ahead, but it's still worth a quick check with us first

Also check you own the tree. A tree belongs to the owner of the land where its trunk grows, and a boundary tree may be jointly owned. If you plan to fell more than a few trees, check whether a Forestry Commission felling licence is needed.

Not sure about questions 2 and 3? That's the most common problem, because tree protection is rarely marked on the ground. Send us your address and a photo, and we check the council's records for you as part of a quote.

The Other Things Worth Knowing

Do I Need a Felling Licence?

Generally not for a tree in a garden. A felling licence from the Forestry Commission is about woodland and larger-scale felling, and trees in gardens are normally exempt. A TPO or conservation area still applies, because a felling licence exemption doesn't override them.

Planning Conditions

If your house was built, extended or developed recently, the planning permission may include conditions about trees, such as keeping them, protecting them during building, or planting replacements. Breaching a planning condition can lead to enforcement. Check your planning papers, or ask the council.

Leases, Covenants and Tenancies

Even where the council's rules don't apply, your lease, title deeds or tenancy agreement may stop you. Some historic estates have restrictive covenants that control trees and hedges. For example, Croydon Council notes that restrictive covenants set up by William Webb may apply to individual plots in the Webb Estate in Purley. If you don't own the freehold, ask the freeholder or managing agent first.

Utilities, Railways and Roads

Never work near overhead cables yourself. In London the network operator is usually UK Power Networks. If a tree is on railway land, it's Network Rail's responsibility. If it's on the highway, it's the council's. If a tree has brought down a power line, stay well clear and call 105.

Dead or Dangerous Trees

These have exemptions, but they're narrower than most people think. Croydon Council says a dead tree needs no formal permission, a dying tree does, and for a dangerous tree only the urgent works necessary can be done without consent, with five days' notice asked for. Keep photographs, and ideally a report from a qualified arboriculturist. See emergency tree work.

What Happens If You Cut a Tree You Shouldn't Have?

Croydon Council says fines of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting down a protected tree without permission, and up to £2,500 for pruning one. More serious cases can go to the Crown Court, and you may have to plant a replacement. The person who carries out the work can be prosecuted as well as the owner. If you think you may have cut a protected tree by mistake, take advice from the council or a solicitor rather than ignoring it.

Before You Cut: A Checklist

  • The tree is on my land, and I have the right to remove it
  • I've checked with Croydon Council, or asked us, whether it has a TPO or is in a conservation area
  • If it's in a conservation area, I've measured the trunk (about 236mm round at 1.5m is the 75mm threshold)
  • I've checked planning conditions, my lease, covenants or tenancy
  • I've checked for nests and bats
  • There are no overhead cables, railway or highway issues
  • I've taken photographs from several angles
  • If I think the tree is dead or dangerous, I have evidence and have told the council
  • Any neighbour who's affected has been told
  • My tree surgeon is insured, qualified and has asked about protection

Permission to Fell a Tree: FAQs

Do I need permission to cut down a tree in my garden?

Not always. You generally need permission only if the tree has a TPO, you're in a conservation area, or a planning condition, lease or covenant restricts it. Check first.

Do I need permission to cut down a tree in Croydon?

Only if the tree is protected by a TPO or in a conservation area, or another restriction applies. Croydon Council can confirm, or send us your address and we'll check.

How do I find out if my tree has a TPO?

Email Croydon Council's development management team, or send us your address and a photo. Don't assume there's none because there's no sign on the tree.

Can I cut down my neighbour's tree?

No, not without the owner's permission. You can cut overhanging branches back to your boundary, but not beyond, and you should offer the cuttings back. If the tree is protected, you can't cut even those without consent.

Can I cut down a council tree outside my house?

No. Street trees belong to the council, and cutting one can lead to prosecution or a claim. Report problems to the council.

Can I remove a dead tree without permission?

Often yes, but tell the council first and keep photographic evidence. A dying tree isn't dead.

Do I need a felling licence?

Generally not for a tree in a garden. A TPO or conservation area still applies.

I'm a tenant. Can I cut down a tree at my rented home?

No, not without your landlord's permission, and any TPO or conservation area rules still apply.

What if the tree is damaging my house or drains?

Take advice from your insurer and a structural engineer first, and don't remove the tree before. If it's protected, apply for consent with their evidence.

Can I cut a tree because it blocks my light?

If it's unprotected and yours, yes. If it's protected, you need consent. There's no general right to light from trees.

Can I fell a tree during bird nesting season?

There's no fixed ban, but you must not harm an active nest, so a careful check is essential. Work may need to wait.

What are the penalties for cutting a protected tree?

Croydon Council says up to £20,000 for cutting down a protected tree and up to £2,500 for pruning one. More serious cases can go to the Crown Court. You may also have to replant.
