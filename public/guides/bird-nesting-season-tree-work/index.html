Tree and Hedge Work in Nesting Season in Croydon

There's no fixed legal ban on cutting trees or hedges between March and August, but it is a criminal offence to damage or destroy a nest that's in use or being built, and bats and their roosts are protected all year. So the real rule is: check first, and stop if you find an active nest.

What the Law Says

Wild Birds

Under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, it's an offence to intentionally or recklessly kill, injure or take any wild bird; to take, damage or destroy a nest while it is in use or being built; or to take or destroy a wild bird's eggs. For a small number of rarer species listed in Schedule 1, it's also an offence to disturb them while nesting.

"Nest in use" is the key phrase. A nest is protected from the moment a bird starts building it until the young have left. An old, empty nest isn't protected in the same way, though some species reuse nests, so it's not always obvious which is which.

Bats

Bats and their roosts are protected all year under the Conservation of Habitats and Species Regulations 2017 and the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. It's an offence to deliberately capture, injure or kill a bat, to disturb it, or to damage or destroy a roost, even if the bats are absent at the time. Work that could affect a roost normally needs specialist advice and sometimes a Natural England licence.

Other Protected Wildlife

Badgers and their setts are protected by the Protection of Badgers Act 1992, and other species such as great crested newts and some reptiles are protected too. Stag beetle larvae live in decaying wood underground, including in old stumps, which matters for stump grinding. If you know or suspect any protected species is present, tell us before work.

Penalties

Offences against wild birds and bats can lead to an unlimited fine and up to six months in prison, per offence, and the person doing the work can be prosecuted as well as the owner. "I didn't look" isn't a defence if you should have checked.

TPOs, Conservation Areas and Planning Don't Override It

A TPO consent, a conservation area notice or a planning permission doesn't give you permission to harm nesting birds or bats. They're separate laws. See Do I need permission to fell a tree?

When Is Nesting Season?

The law doesn't set dates. The period most guidance uses is 1 March to 31 August, because that's when most UK garden birds nest, but it's a guide, not a rule.

Why it's only a guideWhat it means
Some birds nest earlyIn London, some species can start nesting in January to March
Some birds nest lateLate broods can run into September
Some birds nest almost all yearWood pigeons and collared doves can nest nearly year-round
Weather shifts timingA mild winter or cold spring moves everything
Nests last a few weeksFrom nest-building to fledging often takes around four to six weeks for common garden birds, but some take longer

So the safe approach isn't "avoid March to August". It's "check before every job, whatever the month".

For farmland, separate rules ban hedge cutting from 1 March to 31 August under farm schemes, but those don't apply to ordinary garden hedges.

What We Do on Site

A "quick look" isn't a check. Here's what a proper nest check involves:

  1. Plan ahead. Where possible, schedule work for the autumn and winter.
  2. Look before we cut. A trained team member inspects the tree or hedge carefully, from several sides and at height where needed.
  3. Know the signs. Birds carrying nesting material or food, alarm calls, birds flying in and out of the same spot, droppings or feathers, and visible nests.
  4. Check dense areas in stages. Dense hedges, conifers, ivy and tree cavities can hide nests.
  5. Leave a buffer. If we find an active nest, we leave a protected area around it and work elsewhere.
  6. Stop and record. If we find a nest mid-job, we stop work in that area, note the location and photograph it.
  7. Return later. Once the chicks have fledged, we finish the job.
  8. Bats. We check cavities, loose bark and gaps for signs of bats. If we find or suspect a roost, we stop and take specialist advice.

If a tree or hedge is too dense, tall or tangled to check properly, we'll tell you honestly and recommend waiting for autumn or winter.

What If You Find a Nest?

SituationWhat to do
You find an active nest before workDon't start. Wait until the chicks have left, or take advice
You find a nest while cuttingStop immediately, leave a buffer, and don't disturb it
You're not sure it's activeAssume it is, and watch from a distance for adult birds coming and going
Chicks have fallen out, or a nest has been damagedKeep the area quiet, and contact a wildlife rescue or the RSPB for advice
You think someone is destroying nestsReport it to the police on 101, or 999 if a crime is in progress

Don't move a nest. Moving or destroying an active nest is an offence, and it rarely works.

Bats and Tree Work

Many old trees, especially mature oaks, ash, beech and trees with cavities, loose bark or ivy, can hold bat roosts. If you see hollows, splits, droppings or bats leaving at dusk, stop and get specialist advice. Bat surveys are limited to certain times of year, which can affect timing.

Nesting Season FAQs

Can I cut a hedge in nesting season?

There's no fixed legal ban on cutting a garden hedge, but you must not damage an active nest. A careful check is essential, and work may need to wait.

Can I cut down a tree in nesting season?

Often yes, but only after a careful check for active nests and bats. Work may need to wait if we find either.

When does bird nesting season start and end in the UK?

Guidance generally uses 1 March to 31 August, but the law doesn't set dates, and birds can nest outside that window.

What is the penalty for destroying a bird's nest?

Damaging or destroying a nest that's in use or being built is an offence, with an unlimited fine and up to six months in prison possible.

What counts as an active nest?

A nest that's being built or is in use by birds with eggs or chicks. It stops being protected in this way once the young have left, though some species reuse nests.

What should I do if I find a nest in my hedge?

Stop cutting, leave a buffer around it, and wait until the chicks have fledged, which is typically several weeks. Don't move the nest.

Can I cut a leylandii hedge in nesting season?

There's no fixed ban, but dense conifers are hard to check and a favourite nesting site, so autumn or winter is best. Don't cut back to bare brown wood either.

Can you do emergency tree removal in nesting season?

Yes. Safety comes first, but we limit the work to what's necessary, check for wildlife as far as is safe, and document everything.

Do bats have a closed season?

No. Bats and their roosts are protected all year, even when the bats are away.

What if my tree has a TPO or I'm in a conservation area?

Consent or notice is separate from wildlife law. You still can't harm nesting birds or bats.

Do you check for nests as standard?

Yes, before any tree or hedge work, in every month of the year.

Who should I report wildlife crime to?

The police, on 101, or 999 if a crime is in progress. The RSPB and the Bat Conservation Trust can also give advice.
