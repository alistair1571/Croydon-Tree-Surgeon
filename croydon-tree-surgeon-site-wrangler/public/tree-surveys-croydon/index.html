Which tree survey do you need?

"Tree survey" can mean several different things. The right one depends on why you want it.

Your situationSurvey you probably needWhat it covers
Worried about a tree's health or whether it is safeTree health and safety surveyCondition, visible defects and risks, and recommended work or monitoring
You own or manage several treesRegular inspection programmePeriodic inspections with a record of findings and actions
Building or extending near treesBS 5837 tree survey, often with an impact assessment and protection planRecords the trees and how the design affects them, for planning
Applying to work on a protected treeTree report to support the applicationThe tree's condition and the reasons for the work
Buying or selling a property with large trees, or an insurer has raised themTree report for a property transactionThe tree's condition and its relationship to the building

What a survey can and cannot tell you

Most tree surveys are carried out from ground level by visual inspection, so they are a snapshot of what can be seen. A BS 5837 planning survey is not a tree risk assessment, and the two answer different questions. No survey can guarantee that a tree will never fail.

Tree surveys for planning applications

If you are building, extending or changing a driveway near trees, the council may expect a tree survey with your application. The standard used is British Standard 5837 (BS 5837:2012). As a rule of thumb, trees within about their own height of the works, or within their root protection area, need considering; the council's local validation list sets the exact requirements.

DocumentWhat it does
Tree survey and scheduleRecords each tree: species, size, condition, category and root protection area
Tree constraints planShows trees and root protection areas on the site so the design can respond
Arboricultural impact assessmentSets out how the layout affects the trees
Tree protection planShows fencing and ground protection during construction

CategoryMeaning

For planning documents, councils generally expect the work to be done by a suitably qualified arboriculturist. We will say plainly whether a job needs a specialist consultant.

Who should carry out a tree survey?

Published survey reports and job listings commonly show arboricultural surveyors holding a Level 4 Diploma in Arboriculture, a professional tree inspector certificate and professional membership. Ask any surveyor to show you theirs, whether they carry professional indemnity and public liability insurance, whether you can see a sample report, and how they keep advice objective if they also carry out the work.

Protected trees and your property

Check protection first

Croydon Council says a tree preservation order (TPO) stops visually important trees being removed or pruned without formal permission, and that you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area. Fines of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting down a protected tree without permission, and up to £2,500 for pruning one. We check both before any work. TPO guide and conservation area guide.

Croydon Council says it is often helpful to get professional advice when completing a tree works application, which is where a tree report can help. If you think a tree is affecting your building, for example you see cracks, one council advises consulting your insurer, an arboricultural consultant or a structural engineer.

Common questions

Do I need a tree survey for planning permission in Croydon?

If your works could affect a tree on or near the site, the council may expect one. Requirements are set in the council's local validation list, so check it or ask us.

What is a BS 5837 tree survey?

A survey to British Standard 5837:2012. It records the trees on and near a site, gives each a category (A, B, C or U) and shows root protection areas so the design can respond.

What is the difference between a BS 5837 survey and a health and safety survey?

A BS 5837 survey supports design and planning. A health and safety survey assesses a tree's condition and risk. A BS 5837 survey is not a tree risk assessment.

Can a survey tell me if my tree will fall?

It can find visible defects and rate risk, but most are visual inspections from ground level and cannot guarantee a tree will never fail.

Should I get a tree survey before buying a house with large trees?

It can help, especially if trees are close to the building or an insurer or lender has raised them.
