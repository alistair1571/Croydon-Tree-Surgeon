Is your tree protected? Rules in Kenley

The City of London Corporation says the whole of the original Second World War airfield at Kenley is a conservation area, and Croydon Council has a conservation area proposals statement for it. In a Croydon conservation area you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree, with fines of up to £20,000 and £2,500. The aerodrome and common are the core of the area, not the surrounding streets, so check the exact boundary on the council's map. The council's statement says the aerodrome straddles a borough boundary, so confirm which council covers your address.

Kenley Common is a National Nature Reserve owned and managed by the City of London Corporation, and its Second World War fighter pens are scheduled monuments. Report trees on the common to the City of London Corporation. Otherwise a TPO is the main protection for private trees.

Access, parking and neighbours

Kenley Common has fine views over the Caterham valley, so some nearby plots may slope. If your garden backs onto the common, check who owns the boundary strip before any work.

If a vehicle or chipper needs road space, Croydon Council offers a dispensation to park on yellow lines while work is carried out, or a suspension of a parking bay. It asks for at least five working days' notice, payment first and a separate licence for a skip on the road. Check current fees on the council's page.

Tell us if a tree is on a boundary or overhangs next door. You may be able to cut branches back to the boundary but not beyond it, and cuttings should be offered back. Work only goes ahead with the tree owner's consent.

Common questions

Is Kenley a conservation area?

The Kenley Aerodrome Conservation Area covers the original Second World War airfield and the common around it. Check whether your road is inside the boundary.

My garden backs onto the common. Who owns the strip?

It varies, so check before any work. The common itself is owned by the City of London Corporation.

What is Kenley Common?

A National Nature Reserve of chalk grassland and ancient oak woodland around a Battle of Britain airfield.
