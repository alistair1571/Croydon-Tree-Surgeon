Types of tree pruning

Good pruning removes the right branches for a clear reason, such as safety, light, space or tree health.

TypeWhat it doesBest for
Crown reductionShortens branches back to suitable growth points to reduce height and spreadA tree that has outgrown its space
Crown thinningRemoves smaller branches throughout the crown to let in light and air without changing size muchDense, heavy crowns
Crown liftingRemoves lower branches to raise the base of the canopyClearance over paths, roads, windows and lawns
Deadwood removalTakes out dead, diseased or damaged branchesAny tree, often combined with other work
Formative pruningShapes young treesTrees with a poor early structure

Why we don't top trees

Topping means cutting branches back to stubs. Arboriculture bodies, including the International Society of Arboriculture, warn it can starve the tree, invite decay and produce weak regrowth that is more likely to break. If a tree is too big we use crown reduction instead.

When to prune a tree

For most deciduous trees the dormant season, roughly November to February, is a good time. Timing depends on the species.

Tree or situationUsual timing
Most deciduous treesDormant season, roughly November to February
Cherry, plum and other PrunusLight pruning in spring or summer, not winter (silver leaf risk)
Birch and other bleeding treesLate summer or early autumn
Dead, damaged or dangerous branchesAny time
Any tree in nesting seasonAvoid March to August where possible

Can you prune your tree?

Check protection first

Croydon Council says a tree preservation order (TPO) stops visually important trees being removed or pruned without formal permission, and that you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area. Fines of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting down a protected tree without permission, and up to £2,500 for pruning one. We check both before any work. TPO guide and conservation area guide.

A neighbour's overhanging branches

Where branches overhang your property you may have a common law right to cut them back to the boundary, but not beyond it. Talk to your neighbour first, offer the cuttings back, and check the tree is not protected or in a conservation area.

Common questions

When is the best time to prune a tree?

For most deciduous trees, the dormant season, roughly November to February. Cherries and plums are better pruned lightly in spring or summer, and birch in late summer or early autumn.

Do I need permission to prune a tree in Croydon?

Only if it is protected by a TPO or in a conservation area. Croydon Council says a fine of up to £2,500 can follow for pruning a protected tree without consent.

What is the difference between crown reduction, thinning and lifting?

Reduction shortens height and spread, thinning lets light through without changing size much, and lifting raises the lower canopy for clearance.

Do you top trees?

No. Topping can harm a tree, so crown reduction is used instead.

Can I cut my neighbour's overhanging branches?

You may be able to cut back to the boundary but not beyond it. Offer the cuttings back and check the tree is not protected.
