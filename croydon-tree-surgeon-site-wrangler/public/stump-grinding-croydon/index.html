What stump grinding involves

Stump grinding uses a machine with a rotating cutting wheel to chip a stump away below ground level. It is the usual way to remove a stump without digging it out by hand. The remaining roots are left in the ground.

How deep do we grind?

The RHS says shallow grinding, to 20 to 25cm below ground level, is normally enough for laying turf, while replanting or landscaping needs deeper grinding, to at least 30cm.

Your plan for the areaGrinding depth (RHS guidance)
Lay turf or restore the lawn20 to 25cm below ground level
Replant a tree or landscapeAt least 30cm below ground level

Hire a grinder or call a professional?

Stump grinders can be hired, but they are heavy, noisy machines that throw out chippings, and they need protective equipment and a check for underground services before use. For large stumps, tight access, or stumps close to walls, drains or cables, a professional is usually the safer choice.

Safety, protection and what to check first

A grinder cuts below ground level, so we check a few things before the machine starts.

Underground services

Buried electricity cables, gas pipes and water pipes can be damaged by machines that cut below ground. Network operators advise checking plans before work and using cable detection tools.

Is the tree protected?

If the tree is still alive, or you are not sure whether a stump belongs to a protected tree, check before any work. Cutting down, uprooting or wilfully damaging a protected tree without permission is an offence. Check with Croydon Council or send us the address. TPO guide.

After stump grinding: chippings, lawn and replanting

The RHS notes that most green waste from removing trees can be reused or recycled in the garden. Tell us at the quote stage whether you want chippings removed or left, and what you plan to do with the ground. For turf, shallow grinding is normally enough; for replanting, ask for the deeper depth.

Common questions

How deep do you grind a stump?

It depends on your plans. The RHS says 20 to 25cm is normally enough for laying turf, and at least 30cm for replanting or landscaping.

Can you grind a stump in a small garden or through a narrow gate?

It depends on the machine and the gap. Send a photo of the access route with your quote request.

Can I plant a new tree where the stump was?

Yes, if the stump is ground deep enough, at least 30cm according to the RHS. Tell us your plans when you ask for a quote.

Do I need permission to grind a stump?

Usually not for an unprotected tree already removed, but check first if the tree is still alive, may be protected or is in a conservation area.

Should I hire a stump grinder?

It is possible, but grinders are heavy and throw chippings, and you need to check for underground services. For large stumps or tight access a professional is usually safer.
