Which council covers your tree?

Four boroughs, four sets of rules

Upper Norwood and Crystal Palace lie within the London Boroughs of Bromley, Croydon, Lambeth and Southwark. The rules for your tree depend on which council your address falls under. This page covers the Croydon side only; if your address is in another borough, that council's rules apply. Check your council tax bill or send us your address.

Conservation areas on the Croydon side

Croydon's local plan lists Beulah Hill, Church Road (Upper Norwood), Norwood Grove and the Upper Norwood Triangle among its conservation areas, and the council has drawn up conservation area plans for Harold Road, Church Road and Beulah Hill. Croydon's own planning notices also refer to an Upper Norwood Conservation Area. Confirm the exact list and boundaries on the council's map before assuming a road is or is not inside one. Croydon Council says you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area, with fines of up to £20,000 for cutting one down without consent and up to £2,500 for pruning one.

A TPO is the other main protection. Report trees on public land to the council that owns it.

Common trees in Crystal Palace and Upper Norwood

The area's tree story is its history: "Norwood" is a contraction of "North Wood", and for centuries Upper Norwood was part of the Great North Wood, an extensive natural oak forest on the edge of London. I found no council species list for today's streets and gardens.

Access, parking and neighbours

Upper Norwood is one of the highest areas in London, and the Crystal Palace railway station sits below it, approached by many steps. Steep gardens and streets affect how kit and vehicles reach a tree, so access is assessed before the visit. On the Croydon side, Croydon Council offers parking dispensations and suspensions with at least five working days' notice; other boroughs have their own rules.

Tell us if a tree is on a boundary or overhangs next door. You may be able to cut branches back to the boundary but not beyond it, and cuttings should be offered back. Work only goes ahead with the tree owner's consent.
