What to do if a tree has fallen or looks dangerous

  1. If anyone is in danger or hurt, call 999.
  2. Keep well away from the tree and anything it touches. Fallen trees can be tangled in power lines or unstable.
  3. If power lines are down or touching the tree, treat them as live. Call 105 (free) to report damaged lines, and 999 as well if there is significant risk to the public.
  4. Keep people and pets away, including from walls and fences a storm may have weakened.
  5. During a storm, stay indoors and do not go out to repair damage.
  6. If the tree is on a road, path or council land, report it to the council. If it is causing immediate danger on a road, call 999.
  7. Take photos from a safe distance and note the date and time for any insurance claim.
  8. Call us. Do not cut or move a large tree yourself.

Who is responsible for a fallen tree?

A tree belongs to the owner of the land where its trunk grows, and that owner is generally responsible for keeping it safe. If an owner knew, or should have known, that a tree was diseased or decaying and did nothing, they could face a negligence claim. A healthy tree brought down by exceptional weather is less likely to make the owner liable. Each case depends on the facts.

Many home insurance policies cover damage caused by the tree rather than the cost of removing the tree itself, so check your policy, keep photos and invoices, and tell your insurer what has happened. If the tree is on council land or blocking the road, report it to the council. See the full guide.

Dangerous trees and protected trees

Croydon Council says a dead tree needs no formal permission, a dying tree does, and for a dangerous tree only the urgent works necessary can be done without consent. It asks for five days' notice of work to a dead or dangerous tree. We photograph the tree and limit emergency work to what is needed to make the situation safe, and keep photos in case the council asks what condition the tree was in. If the tree is protected, we tell you what notice is needed. See the TPO guide.

Common questions

What counts as a tree emergency?

A tree or branch that has fallen onto a building, road or path, or that is split, leaning or hanging and likely to fall. If anyone is in danger, call 999 first.

What should I do while I wait for help?

Keep people and pets well away, stay clear of any power lines and take photos from a safe distance. If lines are down, call 105.

Who pays if a tree falls on my property?

It depends on the facts. A tree's owner is generally responsible for keeping it safe, but a healthy tree brought down by exceptional weather is less likely to make the owner liable. Check your insurance.

Does my home insurance cover a fallen tree?

Many policies cover damage caused by the tree rather than the cost of removing the tree itself. Check your policy and keep photos and invoices.

Can you remove a protected tree in an emergency?

Croydon Council says only the urgent necessary works can be done on a dangerous protected tree without consent, and asks for five days' notice for work to a dead or dangerous tree.

The tree is on the road or council land. Who do I call?

Report it to the council. If it is causing immediate danger on a road, call 999.
