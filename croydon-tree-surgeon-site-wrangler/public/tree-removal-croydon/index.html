What tree removal involves

Removal means cutting a tree down and clearing it from your property. How it is done depends on the tree's size and condition and on what is around it. In many Croydon gardens space is tight, so the job is planned before the first cut.

MethodWhen it is used
Straight fellingClear space for the tree to fall safely
Sectional dismantlingHouses, fences, cables or neighbouring trees leave no room; a climber takes the tree down from the top and lowers each piece under control
Rigging and loweringHeavier sections are lowered on ropes to a safe landing area
Platform or craneLarge trees, or no safe drop zone, where the tree surgeon has this available
Pruning insteadWhere reducing or lifting the crown solves the problem

Felling a tree whole is generally quicker and cheaper where space allows; sectional work is used when the surroundings do not leave that option.

When removal is the right answer

  • The tree is dead, dying or diseased
  • It is dangerous or damaged, for example after a storm
  • It is too close to a building, drain or boundary for pruning to manage
  • It blocks planned building work or access
  • It is the wrong tree in the wrong place and you want a replacement

Removal is not always necessary. If pruning would solve the problem we will say so. See tree pruning or arrange a tree survey first.

Is your tree protected?

Check protection first

Croydon Council says a tree preservation order (TPO) stops visually important trees being removed or pruned without formal permission, and that you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area. Fines of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting down a protected tree without permission, and up to £2,500 for pruning one. We check both before any work. TPO guide and conservation area guide.

Dead, dying or dangerous trees

Croydon Council says a dead tree needs no formal permission, a dying tree does, and for a dangerous tree only the urgent works necessary can be done without consent, with five days' notice asked for work to a dead or dangerous tree. If you are not sure whether a tree is protected, send us the address and we'll help you check, or email the council's development management team at development.management@croydon.gov.uk.

Nesting birds and bats

Wild birds and their active nests are protected all year, and the main nesting season generally runs from March to August. It is an offence to intentionally damage or destroy a nest that is in use or being built. Many trees also host bats, which are protected. We check for active nests and signs of bats before felling, and an active nest can mean part of the work waits. Nesting season guide.

How the job works

  1. Tell us about it. Use the calculator above, or call, with photos and your postcode.
  2. Site visit and quote. The tree surgeon looks at the job and sends an itemised written quote.
  3. Protection and wildlife checks. Tree preservation orders, conservation areas, nests and bats are checked first.
  4. Plan. Date, method, where vehicles go and what to move.
  5. The work. A qualified, insured team does the job and protects your property and your neighbours'.
  6. Clean-up. Waste is cleared and the area left tidy.

After the tree is gone

Removing a tree leaves a stump unless it is ground out. Stump grinding takes it below ground level so you can lawn, plant or build over the area. Ask at the quote stage about logs, woodchip and replacement planting. Where permission is granted to fell a protected tree, the council may ask for a replacement.

Common questions

Do I need permission to remove a tree in Croydon?

Only if the tree is protected by a tree preservation order or is in a conservation area. Croydon Council can confirm; send us the address and we'll help you check.

Can I remove a tree in a conservation area?

You must give Croydon Council six weeks' written notice first, and a fine of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting one down without consent.

Can you remove a tree close to my house or conservatory?

Usually yes, using sectional dismantling or other controlled methods. See our page on trees near buildings.

Do you remove the stump too?

Stump grinding is a separate service, and it can be done in the same visit if you ask at the quote stage.

Can I remove a tree on my neighbour's land?

Not without the owner's consent. A tree belongs to the owner of the land where its trunk grows.

Can trees be removed during bird nesting season?

Active nests are protected, so we check first and may delay the work if we find one. Urgent safety work is handled case by case.
