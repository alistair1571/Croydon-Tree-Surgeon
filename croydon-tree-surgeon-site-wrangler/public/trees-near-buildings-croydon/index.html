Before starting, the team assesses where pieces can safely go, what is below and beside the tree (roofs, glass, walls, cables, neighbours' property) and how people and equipment will get in.

Tree roots, subsidence and drains

A tree surgeon can tell you about the tree: its species, size, condition and how close it stands. They cannot tell you why a wall is cracking or a drain is blocked. That takes an engineer, your insurer or a drainage specialist.

Cracks in walls or floors

Contact your buildings insurer and consider a structural engineer. In subsidence cases an engineer investigates the damage, foundations, soil and roots, and a tree report then recommends what tree work, if any, is needed. Before a tree is removed the risk of ground heave can also be considered, so please do not remove a tree you suspect of affecting your building before you have taken advice, and never assume that removing a tree will fix subsidence.

Roots and drains

Tree roots can be one cause of blocked or damaged drains. You are generally responsible for your own private drain up to your boundary, while lateral drains and sewers belong to the sewerage company. A drainage specialist can investigate; we can advise on the tree.

Permission and neighbours

Check protection first

Croydon Council says a tree preservation order (TPO) stops visually important trees being removed or pruned without formal permission, and that you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area. Fines of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting down a protected tree without permission, and up to £2,500 for pruning one. We check both before any work. TPO guide and conservation area guide.

A tree belongs to the owner of the land where its trunk grows, and a boundary tree may be jointly owned. We can only work on a tree with its owner's consent, and will not enter a neighbour's land without permission. If the tree is on council land or the highway, report it to the council.

Common questions

Can you remove a tree right next to my house?

Yes, using the right method for the site, such as sectional dismantling where the tree is too close to fell whole.

My walls are cracking and there is a tree nearby. What should I do?

Contact your buildings insurer and consider a structural engineer. We can tell you about the tree, but not what is causing the cracks, and you should not remove a tree you suspect of affecting your building before taking advice.

Can tree roots damage my drains?

Tree roots can be one cause of blocked or damaged drains. Who is responsible depends on the pipe, so a drainage specialist or your sewerage company can help.

Will my buildings insurance cover tree work?

It may cover some costs if a tree has damaged your property. Check your policy and keep photos and quotes.

What if the tree is on my neighbour's land?

We can only work on a tree with its owner's consent.
