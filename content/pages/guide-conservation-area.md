## The short answers {-}

- **What is the rule?** Six weeks' written notice before cutting down or pruning most trees. [The rule](#the-rule).
- **Is my road in one?** Croydon has 21. [The list](#the-21-areas).
- **Are small trees included?** The usual line is a stem over 75 mm across. [Sizes](#which-trees-count).
- **Does it cost anything?** Croydon says no. [How to give notice](#how-to-give-notice).
- **Can the council say no?** Not to the notice, but it can make a TPO. [What happens next](#what-happens-next).


## The rule in one minute {chip=The rule}

Trees in a conservation area are protected by the designation itself, even when no Tree Preservation Order (TPO) exists. Before you cut down, top, prune or uproot one, you must give the council written notice and wait six weeks. Croydon's page carries the same penalties as a TPO: up to £20,000 for destroying or cutting down a tree without consent and up to £2,500 for pruning. Section 211 of the Town and Country Planning Act is the legal basis, and the notice is often called a section 211 notice.


## Croydon's 21 conservation areas {chip=The 21 areas}

Croydon's announcement of its review says it has designated 21 since the late 1960s. They are: Bradmore Green, Parish Church, Harold Road, The Waldrons, Addington Village, Upper Woodcote Village, Church Road, Central Croydon, Webb Estate, Upper Norwood Triangle, South Norwood, Kenley Aerodrome, East India Estate, Chatsworth Road, Norwood Grove, St Bernards, Wellesley Road, Beulah Hill, Church Street, Croham Manor Road and Norbury Estate.

**Boundaries are not always the road.** An area can cover one side of a street, and a conservation area can include parkland or an airfield but not the houses beside it, as at Kenley. Near the borough edge at Crystal Palace and Norbury, check which council covers your exact address.

**New areas are being considered.** Croydon has said nine places may be assessed: Whitgift Estate, Croham Park Estate, South End/Ye Old Market, St Peter's Road, London Road, Fairfield Civic Quarter, Sanderstead Village, Brighton Road in Purley and Court Avenue in Coulsdon. The council says it will research each, then ask residents. No dates have been given, and until an area is designated, the notice rule does not apply to it.


## How to check whether you are in one {chip=How to check}

1. **Ask the council.** Email development.management@croydon.gov.uk or phone 020 8726 6800 with your address.
2. **Use Croydon's conservation area map and directory** on its website.
3. **Check your property papers.** Designation is normally recorded in local searches, so your conveyancer should know.
4. **Ask us.** We check on every quote and tell you in writing.

Check conservation area status and TPO status together, because a tree can be in a conservation area and carry a TPO. See [tree preservation orders](/guides/tree-preservation-orders-croydon/).


## Which trees count

- **The general threshold** in Croydon's conservation area guidance is a trunk of 75 mm across at 1.5 m above the ground. Nationally, work to thin a tree to help others grow is generally given a 100 mm threshold. Croydon's own tree page does not state either size, so ask the council if the tree is close to the line.
- **Measuring by tape.** Wrap a tape around the trunk at 1.5 m. A 75 mm diameter is about 236 mm round, and 100 mm is about 314 mm round. For a tree with several stems, measure each one.
- **Hedges.** A garden hedge is not covered as such, but a tree growing in a hedge can be.
- **Roots.** Cutting roots can count as damage.
- **Small, young and self-seeded trees** below the threshold are generally outside the rule, but if you are unsure, ask rather than cut.


## What work needs notice {chip=What needs notice}

Anything that would need consent if the tree had a TPO: felling, topping, lopping or pruning (including crown reductions, lifts and thins), uprooting, and wilful damage including root cutting. Light pruning is not automatically exempt. See [tree pruning](/tree-pruning-croydon/) and [tree removal](/tree-removal-croydon/).


## How to give notice

1. **Complete the application form for tree work.** Croydon recommends the Planning Portal, which also offers printable forms.
2. **Include:** your full address, the species or a description, trunk size if you know it, exactly what you propose to do, why, photographs, a sketch of where the tree stands, and any reports (an arborist's, engineer's or insurer's).
3. **Pay nothing.** Croydon's tree pages mention no fee for notice.
4. **Keep your proof of sending.** The six weeks run from when the council receives a valid notice.

We prepare and submit notices for our customers and describe the work precisely, because vague wording leads to delay.


## What the council can do with your notice {chip=What happens next}

| Outcome | What it means |
|---|---|
| **No objection** | You may proceed after six weeks, or sooner if the council tells you in writing it has no objection |
| **A TPO is made** | The tree is now protected, and you must apply for consent. Your notice does not become consent |
| **Silence** | Once the six weeks pass, you can generally go ahead |

Two details from the national rules, as summarised by other councils and planning guides (confirm with Croydon):

- The council cannot refuse a notice or attach conditions, so there is no appeal, although it can make a TPO.
- Work should generally be done within two years of the notice. Check with the council if yours is older.


## Dead, dying and dangerous trees {chip=Dead and dangerous}

| Tree | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Dead** | No formal permission, but Croydon asks for five days' notice before work. Keep photographs |
| **Dying** | Treat as a living tree: give the six weeks' notice |
| **Dangerous** | Carry out only the urgent work needed to remove the danger, give notice as soon as you can, and keep photographs |
| **Dead branches on a living tree** | Generally exempt from notice, but check first |

In a genuine emergency, such as a limb across a road or house, make it safe first and tell the council afterwards with your photos. See our [emergency page](/emergency-tree-surgeon-croydon/) and the guide on [storm damage](/guides/storm-damaged-tree-who-is-responsible/).


## Conservation area, TPO or Article 4: what is the difference? {chip=TPO or Article 4}

- **Conservation area notice.** A screening step before work on a tree. The council cannot refuse it.
- **TPO.** A specific order on a specific tree. You apply for consent, which can be granted, refused or conditioned, and there is a right of appeal. Details are in our [TPO guide](/guides/tree-preservation-orders-croydon/).
- **Article 4 direction.** Some conservation areas, such as Chatsworth Road and The Waldrons, have extra planning controls over things like walls, fences and hard surfaces. They are separate from tree rules, but matter if your tree job involves a new drive or boundary.


## Practical tips

- **Build the six weeks into your plan.** Pruning for a garden party or a building job needs notice before you book the date.
- **Nesting season still applies.** Notice does not override wildlife law. See the [nesting season guide](/guides/bird-nesting-season-tree-work/).
- **Tell your neighbours** if the tree is on a boundary. It is rarely a legal requirement, but it avoids disputes. See [do I need permission to fell a tree](/guides/do-i-need-permission-to-fell-a-tree/).
- **Explain your reasons.** A notice with photographs and a clear reason, such as damage or disease, is easier for the council to assess than a bare request to fell.
- **Get quotes while the notice runs.** You can price the work during the six weeks. See [tree removal cost](/guides/tree-removal-cost-croydon/).

:::faq Conservation area tree work in Croydon: frequently asked questions
### Do I need permission to prune a tree in a conservation area in Croydon?
You need to give the council six weeks' written notice before most pruning or felling. There is no fee.

### How many conservation areas does Croydon have?
Twenty-one, according to the council. The list is above.

### Is my house in a conservation area?
Email development.management@croydon.gov.uk with your address, check the council's map, or ask us. Not every road in an area's name is inside it.

### Do small trees need notice?
Generally not below about 75 mm across at 1.5 m. Croydon does not state the threshold on its tree page, so confirm for borderline trees.

### Can the council refuse my notice?
No, but it can make a TPO on the tree, in which case you must apply for consent.

### What if I cut the tree without notice?
A fine of up to £20,000 for felling or destroying one and up to £2,500 for pruning, as Croydon states, plus replacement planting.

### Can I cut a dead tree in a conservation area?
Croydon says no formal permission is needed for a dead tree, but give five days' notice and keep photographs.

### What about a dangerous tree?
Do only the urgent work needed to remove the danger, tell the council and keep photographs.

### Does a hedge need notice?
A garden hedge is not covered as such, but a tree growing in a hedge can be.

### Is the notice valid forever?
Not usually. National guidance says work should generally be done within two years.

### Will my road become a conservation area?
Possibly. Croydon is considering nine new areas, including Whitgift Estate, Sanderstead Village, Brighton Road in Purley and Court Avenue in Coulsdon, but gives no dates.

### Who prepares the notice?
We do. Tell us the address and the tree, and we confirm status, draft the notice and quote the job.
