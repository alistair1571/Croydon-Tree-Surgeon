## Does the tree really need to come down?

Removal is permanent, so it is worth being sure. A good tree surgeon will tell you when a smaller job will do.

| What you are seeing | Often the right answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Branches touching the roof, blocking light or hanging over the neighbours | [Pruning or a crown reduction](/tree-pruning-croydon/) | The tree is healthy, it just needs shaping |
| A tree you are unsure about, or you are buying, selling or building | A [tree survey](/tree-surveys-croydon/) | A written report on health and risk before you decide |
| Dead, rotten or hollow trunk, a new lean, lifted soil, large cracks | Removal | The structure has failed, or is about to |
| Dead branches in an otherwise healthy tree | Deadwood removal | The tree can stay |
| Ash trees with thin crowns and dying shoots | A survey, then probably removal | See [ash dieback for Croydon owners](/guides/ash-dieback-croydon/) |
| A tree close to the house and a worry about roots | A survey first, not an axe | On clay, removing a big tree can cause movement of its own. See [trees near buildings](/trees-near-buildings-croydon/) |

If the answer is removal, the rest of this page tells you what to expect.


## Is removal allowed in Croydon?

The full rules are in our guides to [tree preservation orders](/guides/tree-preservation-orders-croydon/), [conservation areas](/guides/conservation-area-tree-work-croydon/) and [whether you need permission to fell a tree](/guides/do-i-need-permission-to-fell-a-tree/). In brief, as Croydon Council puts it:

- **Protected by a TPO:** get consent before anything is cut. Felling a protected tree without it can mean a fine of up to £20,000, and the council charges nothing to apply.
- **In a conservation area:** the council must have six weeks' written notice before work starts. Croydon has 21 of these areas.
- **Dead:** a dead tree under a TPO does not need formal permission. We photograph it first, because a tree in winter can look dead and not be.
- **Dying:** consent is still needed.
- **Dangerous:** only the urgent work that is necessary, with five days' notice to the council.
- **Neither protected nor in a conservation area:** you can usually go ahead, with the landowner's agreement.

**The felling licence worry.** People sometimes fear they need a Forestry Commission felling licence. For trees in private gardens you do not. The official guidance says so, but it also says other protections such as a TPO or conservation area still apply.

**Not sure which applies?** Send us the address. We email Croydon's Development Management team to check, and we do it for every quote.


## How a tree is taken down

How the job is done decides how long it takes and what it costs, which is why two trees of the same height can have very different prices.

| Method | Used when | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Straight felling** | There is clear space all round for the tree to fall | Quickest and cheapest. Rare in town gardens |
| **Sectional dismantling** | Buildings, fences, wires or neighbours are in the way, which is most Croydon gardens | A climber takes the tree down in pieces, each one lowered on ropes. Slower and costs more, but nothing lands where it shouldn't |
| **Crane or mobile platform** | The tree is too big or too close to a building to climb safely, or access is poor | Costs more, but can be the safest route. A crane used on or over the public road needs a licence from Croydon Council, and mobile crane licences are for one day at a time, so ask your tree surgeon how it will be handled |

**Access matters as much as height.** If the only way to the tree is through the house or over fences, every branch has to be carried out by hand. Parking counts as well: a bay suspension may be needed when our vehicles can't stop close by, and Croydon Council wants at least 10 working days' notice for it.

**Near power lines?** We do not cut near lines without the network operator. UK Power Networks offers a free site visit through its online form, and says it aims to contact you within 10 working days. The tree surgeon has to say how long the power must be off, and the longest typical shutdown is 8 hours. Neighbours may need warning because their supply could go off too.

## What happens on the day

1. **Setting up.** We protect the lawn, patio and any access route, and mark out a safe drop zone.
2. **The climb.** A qualified climber takes the tree down from the top in sections, or the tree is felled whole if there is space.
3. **Processing.** Branches go through the chipper. The trunk is cut to lengths, stacked for you or taken away as agreed.
4. **Clear-up.** We rake and sweep, and leave the site as we found it.
5. **The stump.** We grind it out, or leave it cut as low as possible if that is what you have agreed.

Small and medium trees are often a part of a day, and larger trees in tight gardens can take longer. We give a realistic estimate once we have seen the tree.


## Waste, logs and stumps: what you are left with

**Waste.** Chipping on site is normal. Anything carried away counts as waste, and under your legal duty of care you must check that whoever takes it is a registered waste carrier. Tree surgeons are among the trades that normally have to be registered with the Environment Agency to carry waste, as councils such as Test Valley explain. Householders who don't take reasonable steps can be fined an unlimited amount, even if the waste is later fly-tipped. Check the Environment Agency's public register, or ring 03708 506506 and ask for a waste carrier validation check. Ask any tree surgeon for their registration number.

**Logs.** Keep them, or have them taken away. Keeping logs and chip can lower the price.

**Stumps.** Leaving one costs less upfront, but it can regrow, rot unevenly and become a trip hazard, and it limits what you can plant or build there. Grinding takes it below ground level. See [stump grinding in Croydon](/stump-grinding-croydon/).


## Removal in specific situations

- **A large tree in a small garden.** Dismantled in sections and lowered, not dropped. Tell us about side access and any fences or outbuildings in the way.
- **A tree over or beside the house.** The roof, conservatory and drains are protected during the work. See [trees near buildings](/trees-near-buildings-croydon/).
- **A dead tree.** Cheaper to remove before it drops branches. Photographs first, as above.
- **Leylandii and conifer hedges.** They can usually be removed, but cutting a conifer back into old brown wood often does not regrow, so removal and replanting is sometimes the cleaner choice. See [hedge cutting](/hedge-cutting-croydon/).
- **Your neighbour's tree.** You cannot remove it, but under common law you may cut back overhanging branches to the boundary. Talk to them first, and see our [homepage guide to neighbours' trees](/#whose-tree-is-it-neighbours-council-and-landlords).
- **You rent.** The owner's written permission is needed first. Protected-tree rules apply in the same way.
- **Before an extension or building work.** Tell us at the start. Planning conditions may protect trees, and a survey can save a refused application.
- **After a storm.** See [emergency tree work](/emergency-tree-surgeon-croydon/) and [storm-damaged tree: who is responsible?](/guides/storm-damaged-tree-who-is-responsible/)


## Birds, bats and the best time to remove a tree

The Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 protects nests that are in use. It sets no calendar, yet March to August is when most birds nest, so we look for nests before a saw is started. Bat roosts stay protected when no bats are in them; if we see signs of one, work pauses until proper advice has been taken.

Late autumn to winter is usually the easiest time to plan removal, because nesting is over. A dangerous tree should not wait for the right season. See [tree and hedge work in nesting season](/guides/bird-nesting-season-tree-work/).


## After the tree is gone

- **Replanting.** Consent can come with conditions, and replacing a tree may be one. Even without one, a young tree planted in a better spot and at a sensible distance from the house is a good reason to remove an unsuitable one.
- **Light and lawn.** A garden that was shaded for years may need reseeding or a change of planting.
- **Roots.** A ground-out stump leaves roots to rot away. Avoid planting directly over the spot for a while, and ask us what is sensible.
- **The neighbours.** Let them know the date and any noise, and tell us if they have shared access.

### How much does it cost to remove a tree in Croydon?
From about £250 for a small tree with easy access, up to £3,000 or more for a large tree, with tight access or a tree over a building costing more. The table above gives the detail, and our [calculator](/tree-surgery-cost-calculator/) gives a starting figure.

### Do I need permission to remove a tree in Croydon?
Only if it has a TPO or is in a conservation area. A TPO needs the council's consent, and a conservation area needs six weeks' written notice. We check both before we quote.

### Can I remove a tree myself?
If it is not protected, you can, but falling branches, ladders and chainsaws are a risky combination, and a tree under tension can move unexpectedly. Most people hire a qualified tree surgeon for anything bigger than a shrub.

### Can you take a tree down in a conservation area?
Yes, once the six weeks' notice has been given and the council has had its chance to respond. We prepare and send the notice for you.

### Can removing a tree cause subsidence?
It can, particularly on shrinkable clay close to a house, where the ground can swell once the tree stops drawing water from it. If the tree is close to the building, speak to your insurer or a structural engineer first.

### Who pays to remove a dead tree?
The owner of the land it stands on. A neighbour who knew a tree was dying may share liability if it causes damage, but each case differs. See [storm-damaged tree: who is responsible?](/guides/storm-damaged-tree-who-is-responsible/)

### Is the price fixed?
The quote is in writing before we start. If the job turns out to need more, we tell you before anything extra is done.

### How do I check my tree surgeon is registered to carry waste?
Search the Environment Agency's public register, or ring 03708 506506. Ask for the registration number on the quote.

### Are you insured and qualified?
Our cover is £5 million public liability, and the climbers hold NPTC/LANTRA qualifications. We can show proof of both on request, and you should ask any firm for the same. [How to choose a tree surgeon](/guides/how-to-choose-a-tree-surgeon/) lists what to check.
