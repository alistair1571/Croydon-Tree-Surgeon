## What Tree Removal Involves {chip=What removal involves}

Tree removal means cutting a tree down and clearing it from your property. How we do it depends on the tree's size and condition and on what is around it. In many Croydon gardens space is tight, so we plan the job before the first cut.

| Method | When we use it |
|---|---|
| Straight felling | There's clear space for the tree to fall safely |
| Sectional dismantling | Houses, fences, cables or neighbouring trees leave no room. A climber takes the tree down from the top and lowers each piece under control |
| Rigging and lowering | Heavier sections are lowered on ropes to a safe landing area |
| Platform or crane | Large trees, or no safe drop zone |
| Pruning instead | Reducing or lifting the crown solves the problem |

Felling a tree whole is generally quicker and cheaper where space allows. We use sectional work when the surroundings don't leave that option.

### When Removal Is the Right Answer

+ The tree is dead, dying or diseased
+ It is dangerous or damaged, for example after a storm
+ It is too close to a building, drain or boundary for pruning to manage
+ It blocks planned building work or access
+ It is the wrong tree in the wrong place and you want a replacement

Removal isn't always necessary. If pruning would solve the problem, we'll tell you. See [tree pruning](/tree-pruning-croydon/) or book a [tree survey](/tree-surveys-croydon/) first.

## Tree Removal for Every Situation {chip=Every situation}

### Large Tree Removal in a Small Garden
Most Croydon gardens, especially the terraces around [Thornton Heath](/tree-surgeon-thornton-heath/), [Norbury](/tree-surgeon-norbury/) and [Addiscombe](/tree-surgeon-addiscombe/), have no space to fell a tree whole. Our climbers take the tree down from the top in controlled sections, lowering each piece on ropes, so your fences, patio and neighbours' gardens stay untouched. Sectional work takes longer than straight felling, which is why access drives the price.

### Dead Tree Removal
A dead tree is brittle and can drop branches without warning, so it should be taken down by a qualified climber and never as a DIY job. Croydon Council's guidance is that a dead tree needs no formal permission, but five days' notice is asked for work to a dead or dangerous tree, and it's wise to keep photographs as evidence. Ash dieback is a growing cause of dead and failing trees locally: [ash dieback guide →](/guides/ash-dieback-croydon/)

### Leylandii and Conifer Removal
Leylandii grow fast and often end up far too tall and dense for a garden. Cutting them back hard usually leaves bare brown wood that doesn't regrow, which is why removal is often the better option. Conifers don't resprout from a cut stump, so grinding is usually optional. If it's a neighbour's hedge blocking your light, see our [hedge cutting page](/hedge-cutting-croydon/) for the High Hedges route.

### Tree Removal Near Power Lines
Never work near overhead cables yourself. Trees close to or touching power lines need specialist handling and, in London, usually coordination with the network operator, UK Power Networks. If a tree has brought down a line, stay well clear and call 105. Tell us about any cables when you request your quote so we can plan the right method.

### Trees Over or Close to a House
We remove trees over roofs, conservatories and garages using rigging, so nothing falls onto your property. Removal isn't always the answer. Where a tree is near foundations, taking it out can sometimes cause the soil to swell and do more harm than good. See [trees near buildings](/trees-near-buildings-croydon/).

### Tree Removal for Subsidence or Root Damage
Broadly, northern Croydon (Norbury, Thornton Heath, Upper Norwood and Crystal Palace) sits on clay soil that shrinks in dry weather, while the southern hills (Purley, Kenley, Coulsdon, Sanderstead and Selsdon) sit on chalk. Clay-soil subsidence claims often involve nearby trees, but don't remove a large tree near a building until your insurer or a structural engineer has advised.

### Tree Removal Before an Extension or Building Work
Trees in the way of an extension, driveway or new build often need removing first. Check for TPOs and planning conditions before you plan the build. Planning permission doesn't automatically override a TPO, and an unpermitted removal can hold up the whole project. We check the tree's status and give you a written quote you can pass to your builder or architect.

### Your Neighbour's Tree
A tree belongs to the owner of the land where its trunk grows, so you can't have a neighbour's tree removed without their consent. You may cut overhanging branches back to the boundary line (offer the cuttings back, and check for TPO or conservation area status first). If you're sharing a tree or boundary, agree the work and cost in writing.

### Landlords, Letting Agents and Commercial Sites
Tree removal at rental properties, blocks and commercial sites needs the freeholder's or owner's permission. We can carry out a [tree survey](/tree-surveys-croydon/) and give a written quote to pass to the owner or managing agent.

### Emergency Tree Removal
Trees that have fallen or split after a storm need making safe fast. See [emergency tree work](/emergency-tree-surgeon-croydon/).

## Is Your Tree Protected? Croydon Council Rules {chip=Is your tree protected?}

### Check Protection First
Croydon Council says a tree preservation order (TPO) stops visually important trees being removed or pruned without formal permission, and that you must give six weeks' written notice before work to a tree in a conservation area. In a conservation area this applies to trees with a trunk over 75mm in diameter, measured 1.5m above ground. Fines of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting down a protected tree without permission, and up to £2,500 for pruning one. We check both before any work. [TPO guide](/guides/tree-preservation-orders-croydon/) and [conservation area guide](/guides/conservation-area-tree-work-croydon/).

| Situation | What you must do | Risk if you don't |
|---|---|---|
| Tree has a TPO | Apply to Croydon Council for consent | Fine up to £20,000 (felling), £2,500 (pruning) |
| Tree in a conservation area | Give six weeks' written notice (trunk over 75mm at 1.5m) | Same offences as a TPO |
| Dead tree | No formal permission; five days' notice asked for | Keep photographic evidence |
| Dying tree | Permission needed | As above |
| Dangerous tree | Only urgent works without consent; give notice | As above |
| Unprotected tree, outside a conservation area | Generally no permission needed | Still check for nests and bats |

### Dead, Dying or Dangerous Trees
Croydon Council says a dead tree needs no formal permission, a dying tree does, and for a dangerous tree only the urgent works necessary can be done without consent, with five days' notice asked for work to a dead or dangerous tree. If you're not sure whether your tree is protected, send us the address and we'll check for you, or you can email the council's development management team at development.management@croydon.gov.uk.

## Nesting Birds and Bats {chip=Nests and bats}

Wild birds and their active nests are protected, and the main nesting season generally runs from March to August. It's an offence to intentionally damage or destroy a nest that is in use or being built. Many trees also host bats, which are protected. We check for active nests and signs of bats before felling, and an active nest can mean part of the work waits. [Nesting season guide](/guides/bird-nesting-season-tree-work/).

## After the Tree Is Gone {chip=After the tree is gone}

Removing a tree leaves a stump unless it is ground out. [Stump grinding](/stump-grinding-croydon/) takes it below ground level so you can lawn, plant or build over the area. Ask at the quote stage about logs, woodchip and replacement planting. Where permission is granted to fell a protected tree, the council may ask for a replacement.

:::faq Tree Removal FAQs
### How much does it cost to remove a tree in Croydon?
Most tree removal in Croydon costs between {{p.removal.between}}, depending on size, access and condition. A small tree with easy access is at the low end, and a large tree in a tight garden or over a building is at the top. You get a fixed written quote after we've assessed the tree.

### Do I need permission to remove a tree in Croydon?
Only if the tree is protected by a tree preservation order or is in a conservation area. Croydon Council can confirm, or send us the address and we'll check for you.

### Can I remove a tree in a conservation area?
You must give Croydon Council six weeks' written notice first, and a fine of up to £20,000 can follow for cutting one down without consent.

### How long does tree removal take?
A small or medium tree usually takes a few hours to a day. Large trees or complex sectional jobs can take longer. We confirm the timing in your quote.

### Can you remove a large tree from a small garden?
Yes. Sectional dismantling lets a climber take the tree down in pieces and lower them under control, without needing space to fell it whole.

### Can you remove a tree close to my house or conservatory?
Usually yes, using sectional dismantling or other controlled methods. See our page on [trees near buildings](/trees-near-buildings-croydon/).

### Does the price include taking the waste away?
Yes, unless you ask to keep logs or woodchip. Your itemised quote shows what's included.

### Do you remove the stump too?
Stump grinding is priced separately, but we can do it in the same visit if you ask at the quote stage.

### Who is responsible for removing a dead tree?
The owner of the land it grows on. For a street or park tree, report it to Croydon Council.

### Can I remove a tree on my neighbour's land?
Not without the owner's consent. A tree belongs to the owner of the land where its trunk grows.

### Can I remove a tree myself?
Legally yes if it's unprotected, but tree work is among the most dangerous jobs in the UK. We don't recommend DIY for anything you can't reach from the ground, or any tree near buildings, cables or a boundary.

### Can trees be removed during bird nesting season?
Active nests are protected, so we check first and may delay the work if we find one. Urgent safety work is handled case by case.

### What's the best time of year to remove a tree?
Most trees can be removed year-round. Nesting season (March to August) can delay work if there's an active nest, so winter and early spring are the easiest windows.
