## The short answers {-}

- **Garden tree, no restrictions:** you can generally fell it yourself, or hire someone.
- **Croydon fines:** up to £20,000 for felling a protected tree without consent, up to £2,500 for pruning. [Penalties](#penalties).
- **Felling licence:** not needed for garden trees. [Why](#felling-licence).
- **Neighbour's tree:** you cannot fell it, only trim your side. [Neighbours](#neighbours).
- **Rented flat or house:** ask your landlord or freeholder first. [Leases](#leases).

## 1. Is it your tree? {chip=Ownership}

Citizens Advice says that if the trunk or main stem is on your land, you own the tree. A tree on a boundary is trickier: your title documents may say who is responsible, and the Land Registry sells copies for a few pounds. Photograph where the trunk meets the ground, because ownership turns on where the trunk stands, not where the crown spreads.


## 2 and 3. TPOs and conservation areas {chip=TPOs and conservation areas}

These are the two checks that carry fines. Croydon's figures are up to £20,000 for felling or destroying a protected tree and up to £2,500 for pruning, and you may have to plant a replacement. The person doing the work can be prosecuted as well as the owner.

To check, email development.management@croydon.gov.uk or phone 020 8726 6800 with your address and a description of the tree, and ask about conservation area status in the same message. We do that for you on every quote.

Remember that a small or ordinary-looking tree can be protected, that area orders protect trees you cannot tell from their neighbours, and that cutting roots can count as damage. Everything about dead, dying and dangerous trees is in the [TPO guide](/guides/tree-preservation-orders-croydon/).


## 4. Dead, dying and dangerous trees {chip=Dead and dangerous}

- **Dead:** no formal permission is needed, but Croydon asks for five days' notice and you should keep photographs.
- **Dying:** a dying tree still needs consent if it is protected.
- **Dangerous:** do only the urgent work needed to remove the danger, tell the council and keep evidence.

A tree that is merely ugly, old or leaning is not automatically any of these. If you are unsure, ask us for a [tree survey](/tree-surveys-croydon/) before it becomes an argument.


## Do I need a felling licence? {chip=Felling licence}

Not for a garden tree. The government's own guidance says no felling licence is needed for trees in private gardens, orchards, churchyards or some public open spaces, and fruit trees are exempt too. A licence is mostly about woodland.

If you own land that includes woodland, the rules are different:

- Trees with a trunk under 8 cm across at chest height are exempt.
- You can fell up to 5 cubic metres of timber per calendar quarter, provided no more than 2 cubic metres is sold.
- A licence is not needed if trees pose an immediate danger, or if full planning permission covers their removal.
- If you rely on an exemption, keep photographs and paperwork. The guidance warns you could be prosecuted if you cannot show it applied.

A felling licence is separate from a TPO or conservation area. Either of those still applies even if a licence does not. Tell us if your land includes a wood.


## 5. Planning conditions {-}

If your house, extension or development was approved with landscaping conditions, the council may have required certain trees to stay, to be protected during building, or to be replaced. Removing them can lead to enforcement action. The decision notice for the planning permission lists any conditions, so look there or ask us.


## 6. Leases, covenants and tenancies {chip=Leases}

None of this appears in council rules, but all of it can stop you.

- **Tenants.** The landlord owns the garden's trees. Ask first.
- **Flats and shared gardens.** The freeholder or managing agent decides.
- **Covenants.** Some estates restrict trees and hedges for design reasons. Purley's Webb Estate covenants, for example, can cover hedges and boundaries. See [Purley](/tree-surgeon-purley/).
- **Mortgage and insurance.** If the tree may be involved in cracking or subsidence, speak to your insurer before felling. Evidence from an engineer and the insurer matters if you later claim.


## A neighbour's tree or a boundary tree {chip=Neighbours}

- **You cannot fell it.** You can cut back branches on your side of the boundary only, and the cuttings still belong to your neighbour, so offer them back.
- **Roots.** Citizens Advice says you can cut roots yourself, but you must try to limit damage to the tree and tell your neighbour in advance if a tree surgeon needs to enter their land.
- **Protected trees.** The TPO applies whoever owns the land. You need consent to prune even the part that overhangs.
- **Dangerous trees.** Report a neighbour's tree you think is dangerous to the council.
- **Hedges.** A hedge over 2 m high may count as a "high hedge", and a neighbour can complain to the council once they have tried to resolve the problem. See [hedge cutting](/hedge-cutting-croydon/).
- **Talk first.** A friendly conversation, with notes of what you agree, avoids most disputes. Sharing the cost of pruning is a common compromise.


## Council trees and street trees {-}

Street trees belong to the council. Do not cut or prune them yourself, even if they block light or a drive, because you could be prosecuted or face a claim. Report problems through the Love Clean Streets app or the council.


## 7. Nesting birds and bats {chip=Wildlife}

Wild birds' active nests and bats' roosts are protected by law, and TPO or conservation area consent does not override that. There is no single ban on felling in spring, but active nests mean the work waits. See the [nesting season guide](/guides/bird-nesting-season-tree-work/).


## 8. Cables, railways and roads {-}

- **Overhead power lines.** Do not touch trees near them. UK Power Networks is on 105 or 0800 31 63 105.
- **Railways.** If a tree is close to a line, contact Network Rail on 03457 11 41 41.
- **Highway work.** A crane or platform on the road needs a licence, and a parking bay suspension needs at least 10 working days' notice and payment first.
- **Gas.** In an emergency, 0800 111 999.


## Trees damaging drains or the house {chip=Drains and the house}

Do not fell the tree first. Get your insurer's and a structural engineer's advice, and apply for consent if the tree is protected. Removing a tree too early can complicate a claim, and removing the wrong one may not fix the problem. See [trees near buildings](/trees-near-buildings-croydon/).


## Blocking light, views or the sun {-}

There is no general right to light from a tree. If a hedge is the problem, the high-hedge complaint route may help. If it is your own protected tree, you still need consent.


## What happens if you cut without permission {chip=Penalties}

- Up to £20,000 for felling or destroying a protected tree, and up to £2,500 for pruning.
- A replacement tree, which is itself protected.
- Prosecution of the person who did the work, not just the owner.
- Awkward questions when you sell, because buyers' solicitors ask about tree protection and enforcement.

**If you have already cut a protected tree,** stop work, photograph what remains, and contact the council before anything else happens. We would rather help you put it right than see it compounded.

:::faq Do I need permission to fell a tree: frequently asked questions
### Can I cut down a tree in my garden in Croydon?
Usually yes, unless it has a TPO, your home is in a conservation area, or a lease, covenant or planning condition restricts it.

### How do I find out if my tree has a TPO?
Email development.management@croydon.gov.uk or phone 020 8726 6800 with your address and a description of the tree.

### Do I need a felling licence for a garden tree?
No. Garden trees, orchards and fruit trees are exempt. A licence is mostly about woodland.

### How many trees can I fell without a licence?
In woodland you own, up to 5 cubic metres per calendar quarter, with no more than 2 cubic metres sold. Trees with a trunk under 8 cm across are exempt.

### Can I cut down my neighbour's tree?
No. You can trim branches on your side of the boundary and offer the cuttings back.

### Can I cut a tree in a rented house or flat?
Ask your landlord or freeholder. They own the garden trees, and you may be in breach of the tenancy if you cut without consent.

### Can I fell a tree that is damaging my drains?
Get advice from your insurer and an engineer first. If the tree is protected, apply for consent.

### Can I cut a tree during bird nesting season?
Only if there are no active nests. There is no blanket ban, but nests are protected, so we check before any spring or summer job.

### What if the tree is dead?
Croydon says no formal permission is needed for a dead tree, but give five days' notice and keep photographs.

### Who is responsible for a tree on a boundary?
Check your title documents. The Land Registry sells copies. Where the trunk stands is usually the key.

### Can I cut a street tree outside my house?
No. Street trees belong to the council. Report problems through the Love Clean Streets app.

### Do you check all this for me?
Yes. We check protection and conservation area status on every quote, and prepare the notice or application if one is needed.
